# Intent

This project exists to help answer a difficult question: what is really going on with the AI transformation of the economy, society, and work?

The starting intuition is that the current conversation around AI is both saturated and underdeveloped at the same time. There is no shortage of commentary, hype, fear, metrics, product announcements, or isolated examples. But there is a striking lack of conceptualization. We are surrounded by fragments: productivity claims, labor-market anxieties, platform narratives, automation demos, organizational experiments, social disruptions. What is missing is a serious attempt to connect them into a coherent understanding.

This repository is an attempt to do that work.

It is built on a second intuition: while the present often feels conceptually chaotic, useful frameworks already exist. They are scattered across economics, sociology, organization theory, innovation studies, political economy, science and technology studies, management research, and adjacent fields. Some describe structural transformations. Some describe mechanisms of coordination, value capture, technological adoption, cognitive change, labor displacement, institutional reconfiguration, or social conflict. None is sufficient on its own. But together, they may help us make sense of the AI moment with more rigor than the usual public discourse allows.

This project is therefore not meant to be a news feed, a collection of hot takes, or a simple archive of articles. It is meant to be a structured inquiry into the nature of the AI transformation.

## Working Orientation

The aim is not to defend a predetermined thesis.

The aim is to investigate, compare, synthesize, and model.

That means:

- collecting serious references that either help conceptualize the AI revolution or document its concrete effects;
- extracting the key ideas and mechanisms from those references;
- distinguishing evidence from interpretation;
- building bridges between disciplines that are often kept apart;
- and gradually assembling a model of what is happening, for whom, through which mechanisms, and with what consequences.

The guiding question is simple: if we strip away the noise, what are the underlying dynamics of the AI transformation?

## Repository Structure

The repository is organized around several complementary content areas.

### `content/bibliography/`

This folder contains bibliographic entries related to understanding and conceptualizing the AI revolution, or to documenting its effects.

These entries should do more than summarize sources. They should identify what each source contributes: its concepts, mechanisms, evidence, assumptions, and limits. The bibliography is the project's research substrate. It is the place where the raw intellectual material is gathered, assessed, and made usable.

### `content/evidence/`

This folder is for analyses of AI's effects on society.

It should capture concrete manifestations of the transformation: changes in labor, institutions, organizations, markets, professional roles, knowledge production, inequality, public life, or social coordination. This is where the project stays grounded in observable consequences and does not drift into abstraction without contact with reality.

### `content/frameworks/`

This folder is for capturing the essence of key frameworks drawn from a pluridisciplinary landscape.

That landscape includes, at minimum:

- economics;
- social sciences;
- organization science;
- innovation science;
- and related fields that help explain technological and societal transformation.

The role of this folder is to extract and clarify frameworks that can travel across sources and help interpret the present. A framework here is not just a summary of an author. It is a conceptual tool: a way of seeing, naming, and explaining recurring dynamics.

### `content/model/`

This folder is where the project will attempt to formulate a model of what is going on.

That model does not need to appear all at once. It can emerge gradually from accumulated bibliography, evidence, and frameworks. But this is the place where synthesis becomes explicit. It is where the project moves from collection and interpretation toward structured explanation.

The ambition here is to articulate a model that is good enough to:

- identify major mechanisms of transformation;
- relate technological change to economic and social structure;
- explain uneven effects across groups and institutions;
- and clarify what is genuinely new, what is a continuation of older dynamics, and what is being misdescribed in mainstream AI discourse.

## Standard of Work

This project should aim for conceptual seriousness.

That means resisting both hype and vagueness. It means being willing to say "we do not yet understand this" when necessary, while still trying to sharpen the questions. It means treating AI not merely as a technical development, but as a transformation that cuts across production, coordination, cognition, institutions, and power.

Whenever possible, work in this repository should help answer one or more of the following:

- What mechanism is being described?
- What framework helps explain it?
- What evidence supports it?
- At what level does it operate: individual, organizational, sectoral, societal?
- How does it connect to the broader AI transformation?

## Closing Intent

This repository is a working attempt to think clearly in a time of confusion.

Its purpose is to build a structured understanding of the AI transformation of economy, society, and work by bringing together references, evidence, frameworks, and models into a coherent intellectual project.

It is, in effect, a research program in public form: exploratory, synthetic, interdisciplinary, and unfinished by design.

The goal is not to react faster.

The goal is to understand better.
