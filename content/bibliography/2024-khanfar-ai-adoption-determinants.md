## Executive Summary

This bibliometric review systematically analyzes 91 peer-reviewed articles on artificial intelligence adoption in organizational contexts to map how research has evolved, identify dominant themes, and document gaps in the literature. Using the Biblioshiny R-package tool, the authors conduct co-word analysis and thematic mapping of AI adoption research published through July 2022.

**Research Questions**: (1) How has AI adoption research evolved? (2) What are the key themes in the literature? (3) What opportunities exist for future research?

**Methodology**: Systematic search of Scopus database yielded 4,775 initial results, filtered through inclusion/exclusion criteria to 91 articles. Applied bibliometric techniques including keyword frequency analysis, co-word network analysis, and thematic mapping using Strategic Diagrams (centrality vs. density). Supplemented quantitative bibliometrics with qualitative content analysis of identified themes.

**Four Major Themes Identified**:

1. **Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning** (47 occurrences of "AI" keyword, 5 "ML"): Technical capabilities, algorithms, system performance. Most frequent theme, showing rapid growth 2020-2022 (24 occurrences in 2022 alone).

2. **Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) Model** (16 occurrences): Framework examining technological, organizational, and environmental factors influencing adoption. Second most frequent theme, with consistent presence 2020-2022.

3. **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)** (10 occurrences): Individual-level adoption focusing on perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Showed growth trend with 4 occurrences in 2021.

4. **Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)** (4 occurrences): Organizational and social factors including performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions. Least utilized despite being well-established framework.

**Key Finding - Adoption Theory Underutilization**: Despite UTAUT and TAM being validated frameworks with strong empirical support across multiple technology domains, their application to AI adoption research is limited. TAM appeared in 10 studies, UTAUT in only 4. Authors argue these models remain "underutilized" given their proven effectiveness in explaining technology acceptance behavior.

**Key Finding - Industry Fragmentation**: Previous studies constrained to specific industries (construction, banking, manufacturing, healthcare) and specific AI systems (robotic process automation, chatbots, diagnostic systems). No comprehensive cross-industry frameworks emerged. This limits generalizability of findings and prevents identification of universal adoption factors.

**Strategic Diagram Analysis**: Thematic map plotting centrality (influence on broader field) vs. density (internal coherence) revealed:
- **Motor themes** (high centrality, high density): Core topics driving the field
- **Niche themes** (low centrality, high density): Specialized topics with limited broader impact
- **Emerging themes** (low centrality, low density): Developing areas requiring maturation
- **Basic themes** (high centrality, low density): Foundational topics lacking internal structure

**Publication Trends**: Exponential growth 2019-2022. Majority of articles (24 in 2022, 17 in 2021) concentrated in recent years, indicating emerging research field. Top journals: *International Journal of Production Research*, *Journal of Engineering and Technology Management*, reflecting operations management and engineering bias.

**Critical Gap - Longitudinal Studies**: Almost all reviewed studies are cross-sectional snapshots. Lack of longitudinal research tracking adoption processes over time prevents understanding of how adoption unfolds, what post-adoption behaviors emerge, or how organizations transition from pilot to scaled deployment.

**Critical Gap - Post-Adoption Behavior**: Existing research focuses on initial adoption decision (adopt vs. not adopt). Minimal investigation of sustained usage, system optimization, organizational learning, or adaptation after initial deployment—despite these being critical for realizing AI benefits.

**Critical Gap - Organizational Culture and Leadership**: While TOE model addresses organizational factors, specific role of organizational culture, leadership commitment, and change management receives insufficient attention. These "soft" factors often determine success/failure but remain under-researched.

   - **TAM Model Theme**: 10 occurrences, individual acceptance focus
   - **UTAUT Model Theme**: 4 occurrences, organizational/social factors

3. **Adoption Theory Gap Documentation**
   - UTAUT and TAM frameworks validated across multiple technology domains (e-commerce, mobile apps, enterprise systems)
   - Yet remain underutilized in AI adoption research despite conceptual fit
   - TAM constructs (perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use) highly relevant to AI system acceptance
   - UTAUT constructs (social influence, facilitating conditions) address organizational readiness

   - **Methodological gaps**: Lack of longitudinal studies, over-reliance on surveys, limited mixed-methods

   - **Outcome gaps**: Insufficient investigation of post-adoption behavior, sustained usage, organizational learning

## Methodology

**Research Design**: Bibliometric review combining quantitative analysis (keyword frequencies, co-occurrence networks) with qualitative content analysis

**Data Source**: Scopus database (selected for comprehensive coverage and reliable peer-review)

**Search Strategy**:
- Date: July 12, 2022
- Initial results: 4,775 articles
- Inclusion criteria: (1) English language, (2) journal articles only, (3) addresses AI adoption from organizational or individual perspective, (4) full text accessible
- Exclusion process: Removed non-journal articles and non-English → 2,051 articles remaining
- Title/abstract review by two independent authors → 158 articles
- Full-text review → 55 excluded (not meeting criteria), 12 excluded (unavailable) → **91 final articles**
- Validation: Two additional authors reviewed exclusion criteria for objectivity

**Bibliometric Techniques**:
1. **Publication frequency analysis**: Documents per year to track evolution
2. **Co-word analysis**: Identifies keyword relationships and conceptual structure
3. **Keyword network analysis**: Visualizes connections between concepts
4. **Thematic mapping**: Strategic Diagram plotting centrality vs. density
5. **Content analysis**: Qualitative examination of identified themes

**Analytical Tools**:
- **Biblioshiny**: R-package bibliometric analysis platform
- **Strategic Diagram**: Two-dimensional plot with four quadrants
  - **Centrality**: Measures theme's influence on broader research domain (strength of connections to other themes)
  - **Density**: Measures internal coherence of theme (frequency keywords appear together)
  - Four quadrants: Motor themes (high/high), Niche themes (low/high), Emerging themes (low/low), Basic themes (high/low)

**Keyword Standardization**: 
- Original keywords normalized to consistent terms
- Examples: "artifical intelligence," "AI technology," "artificial intelligence (ai)" → "Artificial Intelligence"
- "technology organization environment framework," "TOE," "toe model" → "TOE Model"
- Ensures accurate frequency counts despite variations in author terminology

**Validation**: Two-author independent review at each stage, two-author validation of final dataset

## Key Insights

1. **Exponential Growth Indicates Emerging Field**: From 0-1 articles (2019) to 24 articles (2022) shows AI adoption research is nascent domain experiencing rapid expansion. Field still developing conceptual foundations, methodological norms, and theoretical frameworks.

2. **Theory-Practice Gap in Adoption Research**: UTAUT and TAM proven effective across e-commerce, mobile apps, ERP systems, yet appear in only 14 of 91 AI adoption studies. Suggests researchers either unaware of these frameworks or believe AI adoption requires fundamentally different theoretical lens—both represent missed opportunities.

3. **Industry Silos Prevent Knowledge Transfer**: Construction industry studies focus on robotics adoption barriers (safety concerns, skill gaps); banking studies emphasize regulatory compliance and customer trust; healthcare examines diagnostic accuracy and liability. While context matters, absence of cross-industry comparison prevents identifying universal principles (e.g., "does perceived ease of use predict adoption regardless of industry?").

4. **Snapshot Bias in Research Design**: Cross-sectional studies capture single point in time—typically initial adoption decision. Misses critical dynamics: How do early adopters differ from late adopters? What triggers moving from pilot to production? Why do some organizations abandon AI post-adoption? Longitudinal designs essential but absent.

5. **TOE Model Dominance Despite Limitations**: TOE framework (technology-organization-environment) most frequent after AI/ML keywords themselves. Provides useful organizing structure but criticized for being descriptive rather than predictive—catalogs factors without explaining causal mechanisms or relative importance. Dominance may indicate field's exploratory stage rather than mature theory testing.

6. **Individual vs. Organizational Level Confusion**: TAM focuses on individual acceptance (will users adopt?), UTAUT addresses organizational factors (does organization have facilitating conditions?), TOE examines environmental context (regulatory pressures). Studies often conflate levels of analysis, making findings difficult to interpret and compare.

7. **Post-Adoption Black Box**: Literature overwhelmingly focuses on binary adoption decision (adopt vs. not adopt). What happens after adoption? Do organizations fully utilize capabilities? Do they adapt workflows? Does AI deliver promised value? These critical questions remain largely unexplored—"implementation gap" between adoption and value realization.

8. **Soft Factors Systematically Neglected**: While technical capabilities (algorithm performance, system integration) receive extensive attention, organizational culture, leadership commitment, change management, and employee training systematically under-researched. Yet practitioner reports consistently cite these as primary adoption barriers.

9. **Operations Management Bias**: Top journals (*International Journal of Production Research*, *Journal of Engineering and Technology Management*) reflect operations/engineering perspective. Business strategy, organizational behavior, and information systems perspectives less represented—limits theoretical diversity and interdisciplinary integration.

10. **Bibliometric Method Reveals Gaps Narrative Reviews Miss**: Traditional reviews synthesize what literature says; bibliometric analysis reveals structural patterns—which theories dominate, which connections exist (or don't), which topics cluster together. Khanfar's finding that UTAUT/TAM underutilized would be difficult to quantify through narrative review alone.

## Relevance to Topics

### ai-economy-labor

This review addresses **organizational readiness for AI deployment**—a critical economic question. If only 19% of organizations have moved from exploration to implementation (per Ramsbotham et al. cited), understanding what differentiates adopters from non-adopters becomes crucial for predicting labor market impacts. The finding that adoption research is fragmented across industries suggests we lack comprehensive framework for assessing how AI will transform different sectors.

### research-to-production

The paper directly addresses the **"valley of death"** between AI research and production deployment. Identification of adoption barriers—technical integration complexity, lack of organizational readiness, insufficient change management—provides systematic accounting of obstacles preventing research prototypes from becoming production systems. The underutilization of UTAUT/TAM suggests adoption researchers aren't leveraging existing knowledge about technology transition challenges.

The post-adoption gap (minimal research on sustained usage, value realization, organizational learning) is particularly relevant. Moving AI agents from experimental deployment to production-grade systems requires understanding not just initial adoption but **sustained integration and optimization**—precisely what literature neglects.

## Relevance to This Repository

This review is useful here not because it resolves AI adoption, but because it shows how incomplete the adoption literature still is. The repository's intent is to connect fragments into a model of the AI transformation, and Khanfar maps the organizational layer that sits between capability and outcome. The paper shows that most adoption research still leans on generic readiness models, narrow industry cases, and cross-sectional snapshots.

For this project, that is a problem statement. It suggests the bibliography cannot stop at "why do firms adopt AI?" It has to continue toward what happens after adoption, how adoption interacts with labor and cognition, and why some sectors or countries remain structurally unable to adopt in the first place. In that sense, Khanfar is a diagnostic source: it tells the repo where the scholarly field is thin.

## Open Questions and Further Research

1. **Why UTAUT/TAM Underutilization?**: Paper documents gap but doesn't explain cause. Are researchers unaware of these models? Do they believe AI adoption differs fundamentally from other technology adoptions? Or do disciplinary silos (operations research vs. information systems) prevent cross-pollination?

2. **Universal vs. Context-Specific Factors**: Which adoption factors generalize across industries (e.g., perceived ease of use, organizational support) vs. which are industry-specific (e.g., regulatory compliance in banking, safety in construction)? Cross-industry comparative studies needed to disentangle.

3. **Post-Adoption Trajectories**: What percentage of initial adopters abandon AI systems post-deployment? What characterizes successful vs. failed implementations? How long does it take to realize value? These questions require longitudinal designs tracking organizations over years.

4. **Theory Integration**: Can UTAUT, TAM, TOE be integrated into comprehensive model? Or do they address different levels of analysis (individual, organizational, environmental) requiring multi-level modeling? How do factors at different levels interact?

5. **Soft Factor Measurement**: How to rigorously measure organizational culture, leadership commitment, change readiness? These constructs resist quantification but practitioners consistently cite as critical. Mixed-methods approaches may be necessary.

6. **Causality vs. Correlation**: Cross-sectional studies identify associations (e.g., "organizations with AI strategy more likely to adopt"). But does strategy cause adoption or do organizations committed to adoption develop strategy? Longitudinal or experimental designs needed for causal inference.

7. **Adoption vs. Value Realization**: Is adoption the right dependent variable? Organizations may adopt AI systems that never deliver value. Should research focus on successful implementation, sustained usage, or business outcomes rather than binary adoption decision?

8. **Rapid Evolution Problem**: AI capabilities evolving faster than research publication cycle. Studies from 2020-2022 (analyzed in this paper) predate ChatGPT, generative AI boom. Are adoption factors stable across AI paradigm shifts or does each wave (rule-based → ML → deep learning → LLMs) require new theory?

## Notes

- **Publication timing**: August 2024 but data through July 2022—doesn't capture generative AI revolution (ChatGPT November 2022 onward)
- **Scopus-only limitation**: Single database means potentially missing relevant studies in other repositories (Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library)
- **91 articles is modest sample**: Compared to bibliometric reviews in mature fields (often 200-500 articles), suggests AI adoption research still nascent
- **Operations management bias**: Top journals (*International Journal of Production Research*) reflect engineering/operations perspective, may under-represent behavioral, strategic, and information systems viewpoints
- **Open access**: Full text freely available, enabling verification and extension of findings
- **Authors' affiliations**: Australian universities (Edith Cowan, La Trobe)—no indication of technology industry connections, suggests academic rather than practitioner perspective
- **Bibliometric method strength**: Systematic, quantitative, reduces reviewer bias compared to narrative reviews
- **Bibliometric method limitation**: Can only analyze what's published—if UTAUT/TAM underutilized in publications, might still be used in practice but not reported in academic literature
