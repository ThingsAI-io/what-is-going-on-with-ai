# Microsoft FY26 Q4 Earnings as Evidence of the AI Buildout

## What This Is

This is Microsoft's earnings release for the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), published July 29, 2026. It is primary-source evidence, produced by the reporting company itself, about the scale of capital outlay behind AI and the revenue the AI business is currently generating.

## The Capex Number

Cash flow from investing reports **additions to property and equipment** — the standard proxy for capital expenditure:

| Period | PPE additions (capex) |
|---|---|
| FY26 (July 2025 - June 2026) | **$115,948 million** (~$115.9B) |
| FY25 | $64,551 million (~$64.6B) |
| Q4 FY26 | **$35,802 million** (~$35.8B) |
| Q4 FY25 | $17,079 million (~$17.1B) |

Capex nearly doubled year-over-year, both for the quarter and the full fiscal year. Microsoft describes its forward-looking risk environment as including "substantial investments in cloud and AI strategy." The release notes that property and equipment net book value grew from $205.0B to $313.1B over the fiscal year.

## The AI Revenue Numbers

Microsoft does not report a single "AI revenue" line. The closest disclosed figures:

- **Microsoft Cloud revenue**: $59.3B in Q4 FY26, up 27% year-over-year. Amy Hood (CFO) opens with this figure.
- **Azure and other cloud services**: up 43% in Q4 FY26.
- **Azure milestone**: Azure revenue surpassed $100B in FY26 for the first time (per CEO Satya Nadella).
- **Microsoft 365 Copilot**: over 30 million paid seats (Nadella).
- **Commercial remaining performance obligation**: $678B, up 84% — a forward-looking signal of contracted backlog tied heavily to AI workloads.

Total company revenue was $90.0B (up 18%), operating income $40.6B (up 18%). Q4 results included a $3.2B gain on the Anthropic investment and a one-time $480M net gain related to OpenAI investments.

## Interpretation

The combination of the two numbers is the evidence point: Microsoft is converting roughly **a third of a $331.8B revenue year ($115.9B) into AI infrastructure**, while the AI-related revenue (cloud/Azure) is growing far faster than the company overall (43% Azure vs 18% total). This is the material base of the AI transformation story: hyperscaler capex is the mechanism through which the fixed costs of frontier AI are being built, and cloud/Azure revenue is the early monetization channel.

This evidence directly informs the frameworks for infrastructure buildout, [platform economics](../frameworks/platforms.md), and the [productivity J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): heavy capital spending ahead of corresponding productivity returns. The output-side of that same spend — what engineering tooling bought with it produces — is documented in the [command-line AI agents at Microsoft evidence entry](2026-murphy-hill-command-line-ai-coding-agents.md), which measures a +24% merged-PR lift from the agentic tools rollout. The operating-cost side of the same AI buildout appears in the [Microsoft token-budget "tokenmaxxing" evidence entry](2026-microsoft-token-budgets-tokenmaxxing.md): within months Microsoft capped engineers' token spend and switched default models, treating AI compute consumption as a metered budget line.

## Limits

- Self-reported by the subject company; no independent verification in this entry.
- "Capex" is approximated from additions to property and equipment; total committed AI spend (including operating leases, pre-paid data center leases) is higher than the PPE line alone.
- "AI revenue" is not a disclosed line item; Azure and Microsoft Cloud include non-AI cloud workloads.
- The press release is a summary; the 10-K contains the reconciled, more granular figures.
