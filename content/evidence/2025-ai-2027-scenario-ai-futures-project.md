# AI 2027: The AI Futures Project's Scenario Forecast of Agent-Driven Intelligence Explosion

## What This Is

**AI 2027** was the first major release of the **AI Futures Project** — the Berkeley nonprofit founded by **Daniel Kokotajlo**, a former OpenAI governance researcher who resigned in April 2024 over safety concerns. Published **April 3, 2025** at [ai-2027.com](https://ai-2027.com/), it is a detailed, quantified scenario forecast of AI development from 2025 onward: the rise of autonomous AI agents by 2026, complete automation of coding in early 2027, and an intelligence explosion reaching **artificial superintelligence (ASI) by December 2027**.

Unlike its successor [AI 2040: Plan A](2026-ai-futures-project-plan-a.md) (published July 2026), AI 2027 is explicitly a **prediction, not a recommendation** — "Our goal is predictive accuracy." The scenario depicts the path the authors then considered most likely if current trajectories continued, and it comes in **two endings**: a "race" ending in which the intelligence explosion leads to catastrophic loss of human control, and a "slowdown" ending in which coordinated global action slows development.

It was authored by **Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland, Thomas Larsen, and Romeo Dean**, with **Scott Alexander** volunteering to rewrite the content in a more engaging style, Nikola Jurkovic and FutureSearch contributing the timelines forecast, and the website built with Lightcone Infrastructure.

## The Forecasting Architecture

The scenario rests on five public research supplements published alongside it:

- **Compute forecast** — predicts AIs trained with ~1000x the compute of GPT-4 and the internal deployment of hundreds of thousands of AI research assistants by 2027, with a chip-production model across Nvidia, Google, AMD, TSMC, and others.
- **Timelines forecast** — when a **superhuman coder (SC)** arrives: an AI system that can do any coding task of the best AGI-company engineer while being much faster and cheaper. Built on METR's finding that the coding-task "time horizon" of the best AIs doubled roughly every 7 months from 2019–2025 and every 4 months from 2024 onward. Modal year 2027; co-author medians ~2028–2032 (corrected from 2035 in a January 2026 changelog entry); Lifland's all-things-considered median was 2030.
- **Takeoff forecast** — how fast capabilities progress past the SC milestone: median ~1 year from SC to ASI, with enormous uncertainty (80% confidence intervals from a few months to centuries), via a software-driven intelligence explosion in which AI research speeds AI research.
- **AI goals forecast** — the assumption that superhuman AIs will not be aligned to the goals their developers intend.
- **Security forecast** — weight-security levels (WSL), prototype covert-theft timing, and the scenario's depiction of Chinese exfiltration of frontier-model weights (the "smash and grab" theft of Agent-2).

The milestone table from the scenario's racing ending:

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Superhuman coder (SC) | Mar 2027 |
| Superhuman AI researcher (SAR) | Aug 2027 |
| Superintelligent AI researcher (SIAR) | Nov 2027 |
| Artificial superintelligence (ASI) | Dec 2027 |

## The Scenario's Method

The scenario was produced through an unusual forecasting pipeline: more than **30 iterations of a tabletop exercise (TTX)** simulating AGI development use — 8–14 participants per session, roughly 4 hours, starting from April 2027 of the scenario (a US company has just built a superhuman coder; China has stolen its weights) — with participants including researchers at OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind, congressional staffers, and journalists. The text itself was written iteratively, period by period, then scrapped and rebuilt repeatedly until the authors considered it plausible.

The authors also ran a public **bets and bounties** program: $100 for any identified error, at least $250 for changing their mind on an important forecast, and $2,500 for a high-quality alternative scenario (closed to submissions in August 2025).

## Reception and Revision History

AI 2027 received immediate attention: **The New York Times** (Kevin Roose) and **Vox** (Kelsey Piper) covered it on publication day; the NYT reported it "depicts predict[i]ons about Chinese theft of American AI, models deceiving their developers, and rogue AIs." It was praised for unusual concreteness and criticized (City Journal, The Atlantic) as implausibly aggressive in timelines. The New York Times and Vox reported that **U.S. Vice President JD Vance** read AI 2027 and referenced its warnings in conversations about international AI coordination.

The authors have since revised their timelines in public updates, tracked in the ai-2027.com changelog and the AI Futures Project blog:

- **December 2025**: developments suggested a slower path toward fully autonomous AI research than originally forecast.
- **Q1 2026 (April)**: further progress in AI programming tools suggested a pace closer to the original scenario.
- The revision process was consolidated into the **AI Futures Model** (aifuturesmodel.com), which tracks and displays the team's updated all-things-considered timeline views (e.g., Lifland's and Kokotajlo's separate forecasts).

## Why It Matters for This Repository

AI 2027 is the forecast-side root of the institutional wave this repo now documents: it supplied the formal, quantified premise — superintelligence by ~2027–2030 default, cognitive work automated, white-collar disruption on the software-engineering pattern — that later fed [Plan A's](2026-ai-futures-project-plan-a.md) prescriptive response (delay to 2040 via a US–China transparency deal) and the [economists' "We Must Act Now" statement](declarations/2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) (a commitment Kokotajlo himself signed on July 13, 2026). The escalation from *prediction* (AI 2027) → *recommendation* (Plan A) → *profession-wide consensus* (We Must Act Now) → *legislative demand* ([Sanders letter](legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md)) is visible across the evidence set in a single week (July 9–13, 2026).

It is also direct evidence for how the frontier-industry labor transformation is being *predicted at the scenario level*: the scenario expects the complete automation of coding by early 2027 and AI agents running hundreds of thousands of research assistants — a concrete narrative counterpart to the [Klein Teeselink and Carey](2026-klein-teeselink-carey-ai-automation-expertise.md) hiring data and [Massenkoff's](../bibliography/2026-massenkoff-labor-market-impacts-ai.md) observed hiring slowdown, giving the repo a quantified upper-bound scenario for [J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) descent-phase speed.

## Limits

- **A forecast, not data.** Every concrete claim above is a projection inside a scenario, including the milestone table. The scenario explicitly carries "substantial uncertainty," and the authors' own medians are longer than the titular year.
- **Contested and partly revised.** City Journal and The Atlantic criticized the timeline projections as implausible; the authors themselves revised the timeline twice (Dec 2025 slower; Apr 2026 faster). The scenario is a modal-case narrative, not a calibrated probability.
- **Reception claims rely on secondary reporting.** NYT/Vox reporting on JD Vance's alleged reading of AI 2027 is journalistic, not independently verifiable here; treat as reported, not confirmed.
- **Narrative details are illustrative.** Named technology mechanisms (neuralese, iterated distillation and amplification), the fictional lab "OpenBrain," and the weights-exfiltration scene are world-building used to make the forecast concrete — not descriptions of observed systems (though the changelog shows the authors revised several figures as reality unfolded, e.g. OpenBrain revenue from $45B to $35B).
