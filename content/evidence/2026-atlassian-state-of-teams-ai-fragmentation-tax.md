# AI, Speed, and Stalled ROI: Atlassian State of Teams 2026

## What This Is

Atlassian's Teamwork Lab surveyed 12,035 global knowledge workers and 173 Fortune 1000 executives (double-blind, January–February 2026) for its annual State of Teams report. The headline finding is the **AI productivity paradox in survey form**: 89% of executives say AI has increased the speed of work, but only 6% are confident they can point to organization-wide AI ROI. Atlassian describes the gap's cost as an **AI "fragmentation tax"** of roughly **$161 billion per year** across the Fortune 500 — faster individual execution colliding with unchanged coordination.

## The Headline Numbers

- **89% vs 6%**: speed up, but organization-wide ROI unproven — the same paradox Marty Cagan (SVPG) and [McKinsey](2026-mckinsey-ai-paradox-where-ai-creates-value.md) describe in their own terms.
- **80% of work happens at the team level**, yet only **24%** of leaders focus AI efforts on teamwork; the report frames individual-productivity tools as missing the locus where value is actually produced.
- **$161B annual fragmentation tax**: teams spending ~6.4 hours more per person per week on coordination overhead in high-fragmentation environments; extrapolated to Fortune 500 salaries and headcount.
- **Only 14% of teams** have "cracked the AI ROI code"; these teams are 5.6x more likely to say AI improves planning/prioritization, 9.4x more likely to say AI increases collaboration, and 2.3x more likely to trust AI-surfaced information.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is the enterprise-software analog of the [productivity J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) material in the bibliography: AI accelerates *individual* output while measured *organizational* outcomes lag. It grounds the "speeding up the wrong thing" argument in a large, dated, methodologically described survey rather than anecdote, and it quantifies the coordination channel through which output acceleration fails to become outcome improvement — a mechanism complementary to the [Klein Teeselink & Carey labor-market finding](2026-klein-teeselink-carey-ai-automation-expertise.md) and the [Microsoft capex evidence entry](finance-of-ai/2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md).

## Limitations

- The Internet Engineering numbers are a commercial survey with a specific Fortune-1000-focused frame ("most AI strategies overlook teams"); coverage of SMBs and non-Western firms is thinner.
- The 89%/6% figures are executive self-reports of confidence, not measured ROI; the $161B figure is an extrapolation model, not an observed cost.
- Reported ROI confidence can lag actual effects, so the paradox may partly be a measurement lag rather than a missing result.
