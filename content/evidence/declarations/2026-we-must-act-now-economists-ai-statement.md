# "We Must Act Now": 200+ Economists and 16 Nobel Laureates Call for Preparation for AI's Economic Transformation

## What This Is

On **July 13, 2026**, a group of leading economists and AI researchers — including **16 Nobel laureates** — released **"We Must Act Now: A Statement on AI's Transformation of the Economy"**, issued under the domain [wemustactnow.ai](https://wemustactnow.ai) and announced by the **Stanford Digital Economy Lab**. It is a short, three-proposition statement signed by **more than 200 economists and AI researchers** from leading universities and AI research organizations around the world.

The statement was organized by four economists: **Erik Brynjolfsson** (Stanford Digital Economy Lab), **Ajay Agrawal** (University of Toronto), **Anton Korinek** (University of Virginia, on leave at Anthropic), and **Tom Cunningham** (METR).

The statement warns that AI could drive economic transformation at unprecedented speed, urges preparation rather than panic, and explicitly names both risks (large-scale job displacement) and opportunities (major gains in living standards). The stated urgency, in Anton Korinek's words: *"Steam, electricity, and computers each gave societies decades to adapt; AI may give us only a few years. We cannot improvise our strategy and institutions in the middle of the transformation; waiting for certainty means arriving too late."*

## The Statement, in Full

The complete text of the statement, reproduced verbatim from the primary source at [wemustactnow.ai](https://wemustactnow.ai):

> **We Must Act Now**
>
> **A Statement on AI's Transformation of the Economy**
>
> 1. **AI may become radically more powerful over the next 10 years.**
>
> 2. **This could drive an unprecedented transformation of our economy, larger than the Industrial Revolution, but unfolding over a vastly shorter time frame. It could bring risks, including large-scale job displacement, as well as opportunities such as major gains in living standards.**
>
> 3. **Economists, policymakers and technology leaders must act now to understand the economics of transformative AI and to build the incentives, guardrails, and institutions needed to steer AI in a direction that complements humans and benefits society.**

An 88-word letter per The Washington Post's count; the statement makes no specific policy recommendations.

## The Signatories

The public list includes more than 200 names; the organizers cite more than 200 signatories. Beyond the 16 Nobel laureates, notable signatories span academia, AI labs, and industry:

- **Economics**: Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, Joseph Stiglitz, Paul Krugman, Ben Bernanke, Robert Shiller, Michael Spence, Christopher Pissarides, Paul Milgrom, George Akerlof, Philippe Aghion, Peter Howitt, Oliver Hart, Bengt Holmström, Alvin Roth, Michael Kremer, Roger Myerson; plus David Autor, Diane Coyle, Tyler Cowen, Betsey Stevenson, Justin Wolfers, Lawrence Katz, Gita Gopinath, Olivier Blanchard, Jeffrey Sachs, Daniel Susskind, John Van Reenen, Benjamin Moll, David Hémous, Klaus Prettner, Anna Salomons.
- **AI research and labs**: Yoshua Bengio, Yann LeCun (Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs), Max Tegmark, Jeff Dean (Google), Jack Clark (Anthropic), Ronnie Chatterji (OpenAI), Sarah Friar (OpenAI), Noam Brown (OpenAI), Dean Ball (OpenAI), Dan Hendrycks (Center for AI Safety), Sam Manning (GovAI), Jaan Tallinn, Tom Davidson (Forethought), Jeremy Howard (fast.ai).
- **Industry and policy**: Eric Schmidt (ex-Google CEO), Reid Hoffman, Vinod Khosla, Jerry Yang, Gina Raimondo (RAISE US, former Biden Commerce Secretary), Arvind Narayanan (Princeton).

Also on the list is **Daniel Kokotajlo** (AI Futures Project) — the [AI Futures Project's AI 2040: Plan A](../2026-ai-futures-project-plan-a.md) scenario, released four days before this statement, provides the concrete plan that matches this statement's call for guardrails.

The asterisk convention on [wemustactnow.ai](https://wemustactnow.ai) marks Nobel laureates; the Stanford announcement says 16 laureates. Two economist organizers' affiliations are notable given industry ties: Anton Korinek is on leave at Anthropic; Tom Cunningham is a METR researcher.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is **elite-profession consensus shifting into the institutional layer** — direct evidence for the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): leading economists, including the skeptics who long argued AI disruption timelines were routinely overstated (Acemoglu, Johnson), are now signing a statement warning that the transformation may outpace the Industrial Revolution's timetable. The concern is concentrated on the *descent and adaptation phases* of the curve — displacement before complementary institutions catch up.

It dovetails with the repo's labor-market evidence. The statement's emphasis on "large-scale job displacement" resonates with [Klein Teeselink and Carey's cross-country hiring data](../2026-klein-teeselink-carey-ai-automation-expertise.md) (-6.2% job postings per SD of AI exposure) and with [Massenkoff et al.'s indication that hiring slowdown may be the earliest labor-market signal](../../bibliography/2026-massenkoff-labor-market-impacts-ai.md). The call for "incentives, guardrails, and institutions" is the economics profession's version of the political brake request in the [Sanders letter to AI CEOs](../legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md) — the same demand, arriving through the academy rather than the legislature.

It is one node in the 2026 summer wave of profession-community institutional responses this repo tracks: the mathematicians' [Leiden Declaration](2026-leiden-declaration-ai-mathematics.md), the journalists' [IFJ Global Framework Agreement](2026-ifj-global-framework-agreement-ai-media.md), the frontier workforce's [Pacing the Frontier](2026-pacing-the-frontier-frontier-ai-worker-statement.md), the [Pro-Human AI Declaration](2026-pro-human-ai-declaration.md), and the [EU AI Act enforcement letter](2026-eu-ai-act-enforcement-letter.md). Five epistemic communities — economists, mathematicians, journalists, AI-lab employees, and civil society — independently demanded brakes on the same acceleration, within the same summer.

The statement pairs with the [AI Paradox: McKinsey reading](../2026-mckinsey-ai-paradox-where-ai-creates-value.md) as an admission that the profession lacks reliable measurement ("we are driving in the fog," per organizer Tom Cunningham) precisely at the moment the transformation accelerates.

## Limits

- **A call to act, not a measurement.** The statement contains no new data; its content is a consensus warning. It does not quantify the expected job displacement or the expected timeline — Korinek's "only a few years" is the strongest quantitative gesture on offer.
- **Signatory counts vary across reporting.** Stanford says more than 200 signatories and 16 laureates; NYT reported "nearly 200" and 15 laureates; the public list asterisk-marked on the site at access includes 17 names. Minor discrepancies reflect counting at different moments; the "more than 200 / 16" figures are used here per the primary organizer.
- **Industry-affiliated organizers and signatories.** Significant signatories hold positions at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google; the statement is not a neutral academic product independent of the labs whose products it addresses.
- **No policy specifics.** The statement does not say what guardrails or institutions it wants, which limits its use as a concrete regulatory program.
