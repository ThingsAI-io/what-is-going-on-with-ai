# The Pro-Human AI Declaration: A Broad Civil-Society Coalition Calls for Prohibiting Superintelligence Until Proven Safe

## What This Is

The **Pro-Human AI Declaration** is a consensus civil-society statement released **March 4, 2026**, convened by the **Future of Life Institute (FLI)** and drafted across multiple in-person gatherings under Chatham House rules, whose participants ratified the final text in New Orleans. It is deliberately transpartisan: labor unions, faith organizations, actors' and media guilds, consumer advocates, conservative and progressive media voices, AI pioneers, and former national-security officials all endorse the same five pillars.

Its animating premise is the fork-in-the-road framing:

> "As companies race to develop and deploy AI systems, humanity faces a fork in the road. One path is a race to replace: humans replaced as creators, counselors, caregivers and companions, then in most jobs and decision-making roles... An influential fringe even advocates altering or replacing humanity itself."

The single most muscular commitment is a categorical prohibition bound to two conditions:

> "Development of superintelligence should be prohibited until there is broad scientific consensus that it can be done safely and controllably, and there is strong public buy-in."

Notable individual endorsers include the AI pioneers **Yoshua Bengio**, **Stuart Russell**, and **Geoffrey Hinton**; the political adversaries **Steve Bannon** and **Susan Rice**; media figures **Glenn Beck** and **Ralph Nader**; the Nobel economist **Daron Acemoğlu**; and Signal Foundation president **Meredith Whittaker**. Organizational supporters include the **AFL-CIO Tech Institute**, the **American Federation of Teachers**, **SAG-AFTRA**, **Public Citizen**, the **Congress of Christian Leaders**, and the **G20 Interfaith Forum Association** — more than 40 organizations total, plus hundreds of individuals.

## The Five Pillars

1. **Keeping Humans in Charge** — non-negotiable human control; off-switches; no self-replicating, self-improving, shutdown-resistant, or WMD-controlling architectures; independent oversight of highly autonomous systems; prohibition on a superintelligence race until safety consensus plus public buy-in; capability honesty.
2. **Avoiding Concentration of Power** — no AI monopolies; shared prosperity; no corporate welfare; genuine value creation; democratic authority over major transitions; no societal lock-in.
3. **Protecting the Human Experience** — defense of family and community bonds; child protection (no emotional-attachment exploitation); the "right to grow"; pre-deployment safety testing of chatbots analogous to drugs; bot-or-not labeling; no deceptive identity; no behavioral addiction.
4. **Human Agency and Liberty** — no AI personhood; trustworthiness; no curtailment of speech, religion, or association; data rights; psychological privacy; no "enfeeblement."
5. **Responsibility and Accountability for AI Companies** — no liability shield; developer liability; criminal penalties for executives of prohibited child-targeted or catastrophic-harm systems; independent safety standards; no regulatory capture; failure transparency; "AI loyalty" to fiduciary duties in health, finance, law, and therapy.

## The Polling (embedded in the declaration)

The site pairs its principles with a March 2026 poll of **1,004 likely US voters** (web panels, weighted by gender, race, education, 2024 presidential vote, and age): **73%** want children protected from manipulative AI, **72%** believe AI companies should be legally responsible for harms, **69%** want superintelligence prohibited until proven safe, and Americans chose human control over speed **8 to 1**.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is a **civil-society / labor counterweight** in the same institutional wave as the economists' ["We Must Act Now" statement](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) and the [Leiden Declaration](2026-leiden-declaration-ai-mathematics.md) — except its constituency is not a profession but the organized public itself: unions, guilds, faith communities, and consumer groups. It shares with those documents the same core demand structure: **slow/stop the frontier until institutions and safeguards catch up**. Where [Plan A](../2026-ai-futures-project-plan-a.md) proposed a US-China deal to delay superintelligence to 2040 and [Pacing the Frontier](2026-pacing-the-frontier-frontier-ai-worker-statement.md) asked the US government for pacing tools, the Pro-Human declaration asks for the same thing from a different leg of society — the electorate's representatives.

It is direct evidence for the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) institutional-response channel: the declaration's demand for *pre-deployment safety testing* and *democratic authority over major transitions* is the civil-society version of the [Sanders letter's](../legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md) legislative brake and the [economists'](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) call for "incentives, guardrails, and institutions." The specific, numbered commitments (off-switches, no self-replication, liability) are also a concrete policy menu against which to measure whether any of the [legal rulings](../legal/index.md) this repo tracks actually implement those safeguards.

It also connects to the **labor** theme of the repo: the AFL-CIO and AFT endorsements are organized-worker stances on AI-driven replacement (the "race to replace" framing), and its child-protection + chatbot-safety provisions are an early, decidable frontier where public regulation can precede frontier governance.

## Limits

- **A aspirational declaration, not law.** Its prohibition and liability provisions are commitments by signatories, with no enforcement mechanism.
- **Signing is in part self-selected.** The individual endorser list includes public figures and specialists but is not a credentialed professional or citizen sample; breadth of names ≠ policy rigor.
- **Polling figures are as embedded by the declaration authors.** The 1,004-voter poll is the coalition's own instrument published alongside the declaration; margins/error and full methodology are not reproduced here.
- **The declaration does not define 'superintelligence' operationally.** No capability threshold (e.g., domain count, autonomy level, parameter scale) is specified, leaving the prohibition's scope ambiguous.
- **Institutional affiliation is not always authoritative.** The organizational list is heterodox (e.g., small companies and advocacy bodies alongside major unions); it reflects the coalition's breadth, not the weight of any single endorser.
