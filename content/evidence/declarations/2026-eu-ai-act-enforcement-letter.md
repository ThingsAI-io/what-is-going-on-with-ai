# AI Researchers, Civil Society, and MEPs Urge the EU Commission to Use Its New Frontier-Model Enforcement Powers

## What This Is

On **July 9, 2026**, a group of AI researchers, civil-society representatives, academics, and MEPs published an open letter to **President Ursula von der Leyen and Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen** urging the European Commission to actually use the enforcement powers the EU AI Act grants it for **general-purpose AI models with systemic risk (GPAISR)** as they come into force on **August 2, 2026**.

The letter's central claim: the systemic-risk landscape has concretized — one year after the finalisation of the Code of Practice, which every frontier GPAI provider signed — and the urgency of enforcement is now greater than when the framework was negotiated. Its signatories point to (a) Anthropic's Mythos model's reported cyber-offence capabilities, (b) critical thresholds nearing for biology and loss of control, and (c) the sovereignty stakes of Europe being cut out of US-anchored frontier arrangements (Project Glasswing exclusion; US export controls restricting non-US-citizen access to Anthropic's Fable model).

The requested action is operational rather than new-law: use Articles 91/92/93/101 actively and early (documentation requests, evaluations, measures, fines), resource the AI Office's Network of Evaluators, and give the AI Office the political backing to act.

## The Letter's Arguments

1. **The rulebook exists and is the industry standard.** The Code of Practice was signed by all GPAI providers advancing the frontier, so it "act[s] as a de facto industry standard."
2. **The risks have materialised.** The letter states as fact that cyber-offence capabilities from Anthropic's Mythos model were "widely reported" and that thresholds are "fast approaching" for biology and loss of control.
3. **Enforcement is the credibility linchpin.** Citing Articles 91-93 and 101 specifically, the letter argues the regime's credibility "depends on penalties being used in appropriate circumstances."
4. **Sovereignty and safety are two sides of the same coin.** European enforcement keeps "Europe's understanding of frontier models and their risks in European hands," complements the European Technological Sovereignty Package, and is "necessary and legitimate" against the backdrop of US export controls and Glasswing exclusion.
5. **The Network of Evaluators needs resources.** The signatories ask the AI Office to empower external assessments under the Code of Practice.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is the **regulator-side** institutional artifact in the wave this repo documents — the complement to the [economists' statement](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md), the [Leiden Declaration](2026-leiden-declaration-ai-mathematics.md), [Pacing the Frontier](2026-pacing-the-frontier-frontier-ai-worker-statement.md), and the [Sanders letter](../legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md). Where the others demand *that* institutions act, this letter is about *how an existing regulatory machine should be switched on* — a concrete operational calendar (August 2, 2026) and a concrete toolkit (Articles 91-93, 101).

It is the direct regulatory counterpart to the [Court of Rome's annulment of the Garante's OpenAI fine](../legal/2026-rome-tribunal-annuls-garante-openai-fine.md): Europe's enforcement legitimacy is contested both from below (national regulators having rulings reversed) and from above (calls to activate supranational GPAISR powers). Together they map the institutionalization channel of the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) — courts and regulators converging on the frontier through slow procedural machinery even as the [frontier itself](2026-pacing-the-frontier-frontier-ai-worker-statement.md) accelerates past institutional capacity.

The letter's cited risk landscape (Mythos cyber-offence capabilities, biology, loss-of-control thresholds) is also the same concrete evidence base the [Sanders letter](../legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md) and the [economists](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) are citing for their weaker claims — here turned into a request for administrative enforcement rather than legislative pause.

## Limits

- **An advocacy letter, not a report.** The risk facts (Mythos cyber-offence, biology/loss-of-control thresholds) are cited as reported/approaching, not independently re-verified by the signatories; they are sourced in footnotes to NYT/METR/SecureBio/International-AI-Safety-Report.
- **Enforcement is prospective.** As of the letter's date, the powers had not yet come into force (August 2, 2026); nothing in the letter measures whether the Commission subsequently acted on it.
- **Signatory list is expert and civil-society, not exhaustive.** The letter reflects a specific constituency (AI-safety-aligned researchers and MEPs), and the companion references indicate a parallel campaign on AI Office resourcing.
- **No quantitative enforcement targets.** The letter asks for proactive use of powers but sets no metrics for what "effective enforcement" means.
