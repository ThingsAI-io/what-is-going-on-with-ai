# IFJ Global Framework Agreement on AI in the Media: Journalists' World Congress Sets Rules for AI Use in News

## What This Is

The **International Federation of Journalists (IFJ)** — the world's largest organisation of journalists — adopted, at its World Congress in **Paris (4-7 May 2026)**, a resolution on AI in the media together with an annexed **Global Framework Agreement**. The framework agreement is explicitly designed as a global political, trade-union, editorial and ethical reference, and its preamble ties the defence of journalism to the defence of democracies.

Its three non-negotiable principles are:

1. **No final editorial decision may be entrusted to artificial intelligence;**
2. **No journalist may be evaluated, sanctioned, or dismissed on the sole basis of an AI decision;**
3. **Journalistic content may not be used to train AI without prior notification, explicit consent, prior express consent, traceability back to the source, and fair remuneration** — including for freelancers and covering both pre-training and inference-phase use.

The agreement also creates an institutional commitment: the IFJ will establish a **Global AI and Media Observatory** (via the IFJ AI Expert Group) to document abuses, disseminate best practices, and support affiliated unions.

## Key Provisions

- **Editorial independence and human responsibility** (Articles 1-2): protection of editorial independence, human responsibility, ethics, source protection, and the public's right to reliable information; the framework applies across a wide range of national contexts, from law-rich to law-absent regimes.
- **IP and consent** (Article 4): a prohibition on using journalistic content to train, improve, infer, or feed AI systems without explicit, traceable authorisation; an explicit claim that "the theft of intellectual property by AI constitutes a serious infringement of authors' rights, the media economy, and the survival of professional journalism"; and dual compensation — for works used in LLM pre-training and for works used in the inference phase — including freelancers.
- **Transparency and monitoring** (Article 7): AI may not be used to replace editorial tasks, to monitor journalists permanently or intrusively, to auto-evaluate performance without human oversight, or to pressure editorial independence.
- **Governance** (Article 8): a living document that evolves with technology and national contexts, with a global observatory to track abuses and support unions.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

The IFJ framework is the **media/labor channel** of the institutional wave this repo documents — the counterpart, within journalism, to the [Leiden Declaration](2026-leiden-declaration-ai-mathematics.md) in mathematics and the [economists' "We Must Act Now" statement](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) in economics, and in the same summer as [Pacing the Frontier](2026-pacing-the-frontier-frontier-ai-worker-statement.md) and the [Pro-Human AI Declaration](2026-pro-human-ai-declaration.md). Where those documents ask governments to act, the IFJ asks its own industry to set the boundary — and, unlike most declarations, it has a concrete enforcement ambition: a monitoring institution and collective bargaining.

Its training-data provisions are the journalists' profession's version of the copyright disputes in this repo's legal subfolder. The claim that using journalistic content to train AI without consent "constitutes a serious infringement of authors' rights" is the same claim being litigated in the [News Corp v. Brave](../legal/2026-news-corp-v-brave-crawlers.md) and [Richner publishers v. Microsoft/OpenAI](../legal/2026-richner-publishers-v-microsoft-openai.md) cases — here asserted as a union standard rather than a legal demand. The framework's demand for *traceability* and *fair remuneration* (including inference-phase compensation) goes beyond what any court has yet ordered, and is the only one of the profession letters covered here to call for (in effect) a licensing/observatory regime of its own.

It also speaks to the AI-economy labor question directly: the "no dismissal on the basis of an AI decision" and anti-monitoring provisions are worker-protection rules aimed at the newsroom-automation wave that the [Murphy Hill agents](../2026-murphy-hill-command-line-ai-coding-agents.md) and token-budget evidence document in software — the first organized-profession contract-style response to AI-driven performance evaluation, and direct evidence for the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) conversation about distribution of gains and institutional response.

## Limits

- **Union standard, not binding law.** The framework is a professional/union commitment; its enforcement depends on national collective bargaining and on affiliate uptake, not on any court or regulator.
- **No measurements.** The agreement asserts harms (IP theft, pressure on editorial independence) without quantifying them; it is a normative document.
- **Scope is aspirational.** Whether individual newsrooms, employers, or platforms adopt the framework is not assessed here.
- **Single-constituency perspective.** The framework reflects journalists' interests; the countervailing interests of publishers, platforms, and AI developers are not represented in it.
