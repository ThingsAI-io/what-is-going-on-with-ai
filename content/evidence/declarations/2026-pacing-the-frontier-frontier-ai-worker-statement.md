# 'Pacing the Frontier': 1,376 Frontier AI Lab Employees Ask the US Government to Build Tools to Slow Automated AI Development

## What This Is

In late July 2026, **1,376 employees of frontier AI companies** — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, Thinking Machines, and Safe Superintelligence Inc., with Microsoft and Mistral also reported — published an open statement called **"Pacing the Frontier"** asking the US government to build the governance and technical tools that would allow a deliberate, coordinated slowdown of frontier AI development.

The statement is short and single-purposed. It asserts that the frontier labs believe they are close to automating AI research, that the resulting acceleration could outrun society's ability to understand or control the systems, and that no single company or country can afford to slow down unilaterally. Its one request:

> "We request that the U.S. government support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development."

The signatories are striking in themselves: the CEO of Anthropic (Dario Amodei), the CEO of Safe Superintelligence Inc. (Ilya Sutskever), the chief scientists of OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic, co-founders of Anthropic and Google DeepMind, and the former co-lead of OpenAI's superalignment team (Jan Leike). These are the people whose own labs' incentives the statement implicitly critiques.

Organizational support came from two nonprofits: **Guidelight AI Standards** and **Encode AI**. Signing is restricted to verified employees of frontier AI companies.

## The Statement's Logic

The statement's chain of reasoning:

1. **AI could create a dramatically better future — but that outcome is not guaranteed.**
2. **The labs believe they are close to automating AI research.** "It is hard to predict exactly how much this will accelerate AI progress, but there is a real risk that capability development rapidly accelerates beyond our ability to understand or control the resulting systems."
3. **Society may need the option to buy time.** "To realize AI's potential, industry, government, and society at large may need the option to buy time to address emerging risks, develop security measures, and strengthen oversight."
4. **Unilateral slowdown is structurally impossible.** "Each company—and country—is under intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow that acceleration."
5. **The missing piece is procedural, not moral.** "Today, the world lacks the technical and governance tools to deliberately pace frontier-wide progress." Hence the request to the US government to sponsor the build-out of those tools internationally.

Notably absent: any demand that frontier development pause right now, any quantified capability threshold, and any specific governance mechanism. The request is to **make coordinated pacing possible** — the pre-requisite for a slowdown decision — rather than to make the decision itself.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is the missing **inside-the-labs** link in the institutionalization wave this repo documents. Where [Plan A](../2026-ai-futures-project-plan-a.md) was a nonprofit's detailed scenario for delaying superintelligence to 2040, the ["We Must Act Now" economists' statement](2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) a profession-level warning, and the [Sanders letter](../legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md) a legislator's demand for a pause, "Pacing the Frontier" is the frontier **workforce itself** asking the state for a brake that competition prevents any single lab from applying. All four channels converged within a month of [AI 2027's](../2025-ai-2027-scenario-ai-futures-project.md) escalation narrative — prediction (AI 2027) → prescription (Plan A) → profession-wide consensus (economists) → insider request (Pacing the Frontier) → legislative demand (Sanders).

It matters for the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) reading because the statement's signatories explicitly frame the frontier's acceleration as running **ahead of institutional adaptation** — "the pace of progress is itself accelerating" (per signatory comments embedded in the statement), the same descent-phase risk the economists and legislators describe, now confirmed by the actors building the systems.

It also matters for the labor thread: these are knowledgeable insiders betting their careers on the claim that automated AI research is imminent — the most direct available confirmation of the [AI 2027](../2025-ai-2027-scenario-ai-futures-project.md) timeline hypothesis of superhuman coding by 2026-2027 from the people closest to the frontier.

## Limits

- **A request, not a commitment.** The statement asks for capability — tools and governance — not a policy decision. It commits neither the companies nor the government to anything.
- **Counts evolve.** 1,376 at access grew from ~1,100 at publication; figures reported by press (Reuters >1,100, Fortune >1,200, Hindu 1,319) were snapshots, not stable values.
- **Signer verification is self-attested.** Employment verification (corporate email/proof) is the only gate; titles shown next to signatories are as provided by signers.
- **No quantitative substance.** The statement offers no probabilities, timelines, or thresholds for the risk it describes; the "risk of over-acceleration" is asserted, not measured.
- **Not unanimous insider opinion.** The statement's ~1.3k signatories are a minority of the several-hundred-thousand people employed by the named companies; dissenting views within labs are not captured here.
