# Bloomberg: ~$70 Billion of AI "Shadow Credit Backstops" Off Balance Sheets Worries Bond Traders

## What This Is

On August 15, 2026, Bloomberg reported that bond traders were "agonizing" over **roughly $70 billion in phantom liabilities** — residual-value credit backstops that sit off the balance sheets of major AI companies but could "materialize at the worst possible time." The article is a market-side aggregation of a financing structure this repository already tracks: the [Nikkei investigation's "shadow borrowing" mechanism](2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md), where SPVs borrow, lease the asset (data center or chips) back to the operator, and the operator guarantees residual value.

The $70 billion figure is Bloomberg's sum of the three deals then public:

| Deal | Backstop | Collateral | Parties |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Beignet** (Meta) | ~$28bn | Data center (Hyperion, Louisiana) | Meta + PE debt funds; ~$27bn package |
| **Sopaipilla** (Meta) | ~$13bn | Data center (Texas) | Meta |
| **Big Sky** (Broadcom) | ~$29bn | AI chips leased to Anthropic | Broadcom backstop; Apollo & Blackstone lenders; ~$35bn deal |

## The Headline Numbers and Positions

- **Nvidia's new lever.** The same week, Nvidia announced a **~$500 billion financing partnership** with six US investment giants (including BlackRock and Goldman Sachs) to fund AI infrastructure for its clients, with Nvidia potentially guaranteeing up to **25% of an opportunity** as residual-value support (Jensen Huang on X). The deal is explicitly aimed at the "circular financing" concern — AI suppliers financing their own customers.
- **The accounting crux.** Meta's filing states plainly: "RVG payments are not probable, and therefore no liability has been recorded to date." That sentence — "not probable," recorded nowhere — is precisely what worries bond investors.
- **Rating agencies move.** S&P Global says it will treat residual-value support as a **"contingent debt-like obligation"** added to adjusted debt; Moody's warns the risk is **clustering** ("multiple such transactions occurring over a short period") creating an "overhang on the company's credit profile."
- **Analyst framing.** CreditSights: residual-value support is effectively **"writing a put"** — "nearly costless in the boom phase, but becomes most relevant in a severe, abrupt downturn."
- **The tempering view.** Janus Henderson's John Lloyd: "You would have to have growth rates of token usage fall of a cliff... They're not trying to hide the contingent liability. They're trying to get it financed."
- **Adjacent data points.** The same borrowing binge: Jane Street issued **$14.6 billion** of bonds (partly for "technology infrastructure"), and Lambda Inc closed a **$926 million** leveraged loan for AI cloud capacity.

## Interpretation: The Territory's Financing Layer Turns Explicit

The blaze of the [AI buildout's financing engineering](../../bibliography/2026-drall-granato-private-credit-state-backstop.md) is now being price-tested in credit markets. The [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) predicts legal and institutional structure lags technical adoption; this entry is the market's own lag measured in yield spreads — investors demanding answers that the balance sheets do not yet carry. In [Dayen's state-backstop picture](2026-ai-bailout-state-backstop-private-credit-life-insurers.md), that evidence entry is the insurer leg of this chain; this entry is the bond-trader leg: the moment the "free lunch" of off-balance-sheet financing starts being priced as the contingent liability it structurally is.

The structure itself is a textbook application of the repo's framing of capital formation in the AI boom: the monetization object is not the model but the [infrastructure and financing envelope](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) around it. What Bloomberg documents is the *explicit* recognition — by S&P, Moody's, CreditSights, and the market — that the backstops are not costless, reversing Meta's "not probable" slogan into the question "under what probability does this show up on the balance sheet?"

## The Figure Is Disputed in Framing

### Fact-check verdict
- **Confirmed as aggregation:** the $70bn is the sum of the three listed backstops (28+13+29 ≈ 70), consistent across Bloomberg's syndicated text and The Edge Malaysia's deal table. The underlying deal figures (Beignet ~$27bn package/~$28bn guarantee; Sopaipilla ~$13bn; Big Sky ~$35bn/~$29bn) match the repo's existing record of Meta's Hyperion exposure in the [Nikkei evidence](2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md).
- **What it does not cover:** the figure omits other off-balance-sheet channels the repo already documents — Alphabet's ~$28.4bn payment backstops and ~$35bn of lease support for Anthropic, Amazon's $20bn facility for Anthropic, Nvidia's earlier reported ~$250bn OpenAI compute-leasing backstop, and the [Nikkei $1.65tn aggregate estimate](2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md). It is a floor, not a total.
- **Not independently verified:** the Nvidia up-to-25% guarantee mechanism and its exposure sizing were not filed in detail at access time; the megadeal's $500bn headline is a commitment framework, not drawn debt.

## Limits

- **Paywalled primary, reconstructed text.** The Bloomberg article itself could not be fetched (403); this entry reproduces the syndicated full text (Yahoo Finance, Business Times) — flagged in access_notes.
- **Market sentiment, not a measurement.** "Bond traders agonize" documents anxiety and rating-agency positioning, not realized defaults or triggered backstops.
- **The figure's provenance is journalistic.** "$70 billion" is Bloomberg's own summation, not a company disclosure or regulator number.
- **Rapidly moving.** Nvidia's August 2026 partnership postdates this article's snapshot; the exposure surface will have grown.
