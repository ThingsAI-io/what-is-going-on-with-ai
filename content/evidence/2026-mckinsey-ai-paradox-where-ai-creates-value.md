# The AI Paradox: McKinsey on Where AI Will Create Value

## What This Is

In its April 29, 2026, Strategy & Corporate Finance quarterly piece (the article Marty Cagan quotes in his SVPG post), McKinsey gives the paradox its most influential statement: adoption of generative and agentic AI is growing, investment is accelerating, but **sustained impact on performance is elusive**. The article explicitly invokes the **Solow Paradox**. On the numbers: **almost nine in ten companies** had deployed AI in at least one business function by end-2025, while **94% of surveyed respondents** report not seeing "significant" value from those investments.

## Why McKinsey Says Productivity Won't Decide Winners

McKinsey's central claim is strategic, not merely skeptical: productivity improvements **rarely expand profit pools** — they set the floor of industry performance, not the ceiling, and are largely defensive. Real value accrues where AI **reshapes offerings, business models, and market structures** and thereby expands or reallocates profit pools (its "three waves": productivity, differentiation, and reduced transaction costs). Early movers, protected by compounding moats and speed, capture the disproportionate value; laggards get squeezed toward commoditized roles.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

McKinsey supplies the strategy-practice account of the same gap the [Atlassian survey](2026-atlassian-state-of-teams-ai-fragmentation-tax.md) and the [Klein Teeselink & Carey paper](2026-klein-teeselink-carey-ai-automation-expertise.md) document empirically: individual/task-level acceleration is not translating into firm-level or market-level outcomes. It reframes the [productivity J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) as a **competitive reset** — the winners are not those who adopt fastest but those who see where value is moving first. That connects the enterprise-micro evidence (Atlassian, this entry) with the capex/credit evidence ([Microsoft](finance-of-ai/2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md), [Nikkei](finance-of-ai/2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md)): if productivity is not the payoff, the $115bn+/quarter hyperscaler capex must be justified by future market-structure value, which is precisely the contested assumption those credit entries track.

## Limitations and Contested Points

- Per-company read: the article argues value is concentrated, conditional, and not arriving on the timeline current capex commitments imply — but also that the strategic risk is moving *too slowly*; it does not predict the capital cycle fails.
- McKinsey's own interest is flagged by coverage: the workflow-redesign recommendations map onto the multi-year transformation engagements McKinsey sells, and its CEO has publicly committed to a 1:1 ratio of AI agents to human consultants (25,000 agents today, 40,000 targeted by end-2026).
- The 94% figure draws on McKinsey's Global Survey on AI (enterprise-executive self-reports of "significant" value), the same class of evidence as the Atlassian 6%-ROI number; it measures perceived value, not measured economic return.
- Aggregate productivity estimates cited in coverage diverge widely (St. Louis Fed's 1.9% excess growth vs. Acemoglu's projected ~0.5%/decade vs. McKinsey's own $4.4tn potential), a difference of ~100x that makes the macro "is it working" question unresolvable from public data.
