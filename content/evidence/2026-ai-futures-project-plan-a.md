# AI Futures Project's AI 2040: Plan A — A Verified Slowdown Proposal to Delay Superintelligence to 2040

## What This Is

On **July 9, 2026**, the **AI Futures Project** — the Berkeley, California nonprofit research organization founded by former OpenAI researcher **Daniel Kokotajlo** — published **[AI 2040: Plan A](https://ai-2040.com/)**, a detailed scenario document subtitled "the least bad plan we currently know of." It is the follow-up to the group's April 2025 scenario [AI 2027](2025-ai-2027-scenario-ai-futures-project.md), which forecast that a full-speed race to superintelligence ends in either extinction or irreversible concentration of power.

Plan A is deliberately **not a prediction**. The group describes it as a **recommendation** expressed as a concrete scenario: a vehicle for communicating and stress-testing policy proposals. Its central request is that the **US and China reach an international deal** to slow the push to superintelligence, built on **total research transparency** for AI R&D (allowing the world to understand what labs are doing and enforce guardrails) and a **"mutually assured compute destruction"** regime — dozens of companies across multiple countries scaling to the frontier together, openly, rather than a handful of labs racing in secrecy. In the scenario, the deal is implemented "imperfectly and only in the nick of time."

The authors are **Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, and Daniel Kokotajlo**. The organization was founded in late 2024 by Kokotajlo, who resigned from OpenAI's governance division in April 2024 saying the company prioritized rapid product development over safety.

## The Scenario Timeline and Core Claims

The scenario works out, year by year, the following pathway:

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 2029 | The US and China agree to avoid a reckless race to superintelligence |
| 2030 | Fully automated AI R&D *would have* produced superintelligence by year's end — the deal prevents it |
| 2030–2035 | Scale within the human range, to AIs roughly as capable as top human experts |
| 2035 | Pause at top-human-expert-level AI to maintain human control |
| 2040 | Unpause and scale to superintelligence ("hence the title") |

The document contrasts Plan A with four alternatives that correspond to how the US could respond: **Plan D (Race to ASI — the default)**, **Plan C (Burn the Lead)**, **Plan B (Fight China)**, **Plan A (Verified Slowdown)**, and **Plan S (Shut it all down)**.

Supporting claims from the scenario and its assumptions supplement:

- **Capabilities forecast**: AI companies will probably succeed at building smarter-than-human AI "within the next 1 to 10 years"; absent substantial regulation, AI R&D will be fully automated within ~15 years.
- **Timeline uncertainty is explicit**: AI 2027's titular year reflected roughly a 50% chance as of writing for Kokotajlo; Plan A's 2030 reflects Thomas Larsen's median; Kokotajlo currently thinks things will go "somewhat faster" than Plan A depicts (see the team's Q1 2026 timelines update).
- **Concentration-of-power concern**: even a successfully aligned superintelligence would concentrate power in a tiny group — "possibly just a single individual" — controlling the world's only army of superintelligences.
- **The scenario's economic model is extreme**: world GDP grows roughly 200x during the 2030s in the Plan A model (footnote 99, which a commenter flagged for the wording "200x orders of magnitude").
- **Incremental policy wishlist** for readers who consider Plan A too ambitious: close the internal/external deployment gap and publish model specs; enforce existing export controls (citing Epoch's estimate that roughly a third of Chinese compute is acquired via chip smuggling); invest in inference-only verification; limit the fraction of compute spent on AI R&D (companies spend roughly half their compute budget on R&D in 2026); AI compute tracking; and improve government AI capacity.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

Plan A is a concrete, dated instance of the AI transformation moving from **prediction to prescription** in the institutional layer — the same shift visible in the ["We Must Act Now" economists' statement](declarations/2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) (released four days later, on July 13, and signed by Kokotajlo himself) and in the [Sanders letter to AI CEOs](legal/2026-sanders-letter-ai-ceos-pause-development.md). Three independent institutional channels converged within one week — a nonprofit's scenario plan, an open economists' letter, and a Senator's open letter — each using different language for the same underlying demand: **slow the frontier down before it outruns control**. This is direct evidence for the [productivity J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) reading that institutional response is now racing to catch the descent phase of the curve, not just the adoption curve.

It also connects to the repo's labor-economics evidence: unlike AI 2027's extinction framing, Plan A seriously models *employment* — the scenario's 2030s economy broadly deploys AI, "almost everyone loses their jobs and starts relying on a Citizen's Dividend," and white-collar professions face disruption like software engineering saw in 2026 (narrated in the 2027–2028 chapters). That is a concrete, quantified scenario of the [large-scale job displacement](declarations/2026-we-must-act-now-economists-ai-statement.md) the economists' letter warns about — and the intensity of the J-curve's descent phase if displacement clusters rather than spreads.

The compute-governance and transparency proposals (inference-only verification, mutual compute destruction, model-spec disclosure) are a policy mirror of the [robots.txt and scraping-litigation entries](legal/2025-ziff-davis-v-openai-robots-txt-dmca.md): the "who can inspect what" frontier the courts are deciding case-by-case, here proposed as an international treaty-style arrangement.

## Limits

- **A recommendation, not a measurement.** Plan A contains no empirical market data; its facts are forecasts (median timelines, Epoch's chip-smuggling estimate) and its economics are model outputs. Treat figures like the 200x GDP growth as scenario-internal projections, not observed magnitudes.
- **Nonprofit authorship with activist intent.** The AI Futures Project is a safety-oriented organization founded by an OpenAI critic; the framework (race to superintelligence, extinction/great-power framing) is contested, and coverage notes accelerationist critics dismiss the underlying "doomer" premises.
- **Scenario claims cannot be verified against reality yet.** The 2029–2040 milestones are projections; early-year narrative details (2027 "Writing on the Wall," the 2028 election-cycle chapters) are illustrative world-building, not forecasts of specific events.
- **Launch reception is the strongest verifiable fact.** Publication date, authorship, structure (five alternative plans), and the incremental wishlist are confirmed by the primary source; coverage by Axios, the Washington Post, and Semafor within the first day confirms the document's policy-notice impact.
