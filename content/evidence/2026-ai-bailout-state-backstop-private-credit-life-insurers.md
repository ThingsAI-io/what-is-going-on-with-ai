# The AI Bailout Baked Into the AI Bubble

## What This Is

On August 3, 2026, David Dayen (executive editor, The American Prospect) published an analysis applying a new law review paper — Granato and Drall, *[Private Credit's State Backstop: How Private Equity Socializes Risk Through Insurers](../bibliography/2026-drall-granato-private-credit-state-backstop.md)* (SSRN 7152239) — to the summer-2026 AI market rout. Dayen's argument: private credit is private equity's ~$3 trillion financing arm, and it is entangled in the AI buildout through data-center loans; those loans flow into life insurers owned by the same private equity firms; and if those loans default, state guaranty funds — repaid largely through state premium-tax credits — make taxpayers the backstop. "If AI is propping up the economy, who is propping up AI?" Dayen asks. "The answer... could be the U.S. taxpayer."

The article is the bridge that maps a general financial-mechanism paper onto the AI bubble. Its financial figures are grounded in the paper (verified below); its AI-market figures are contemporaneous reporting that this entry records as-reported.

## The AI Market Context (as reported by Dayen)

- **Aschenbrenner's fund.** Leopold Aschenbrenner's "Situational Awareness" fund, worth **$45 billion** at its height, sold nearly its entire stock portfolio to Citadel Securities in late July 2026. It was up **439% on the year** as recently as a month earlier, then dropped **67% in July** in the AI stock rout.
- **Broader rout.** The Nasdaq entered its **second correction of 2026** (≥10% off its peak); SpaceX has lost **the equivalent of the entire market capitalization of Tesla** since its post-IPO high in June 2026 and is still falling.
- **The private-credit connection.** Private credit has lent heavily to AI data-center construction. As construction delays grow — **~530 local jurisdictions** and counting have passed data-center restrictions or bans — and AI returns disappoint, those loans "could go into mass default," with higher interest rates an additional stress point and investor redemption requests rising.

## The Mechanism: Life Insurers as the Dumping Ground

- Private equity owns **at least $1.5 trillion in life insurance company assets**. Apollo bought Athene (2022); KKR acquired Global Atlantic (2020).
- Life insurers hold long-duration policyholder capital that PE deploys into "underperforming private credit loans, rather than the boring mix of Treasurys and corporate bonds."
- After acquisition, a PE firm **cuts the insurer's internal investment staff in half on average**, and drops it entirely about a third of the time, replacing it with asset-management fees paid to the PE parent's affiliates.
- In **44 states**, life insurers can take a **tax credit for guaranty-fund assessments** and be repaid 100% over time — converting the industry-funded safety net into a state-funded one.

## The Insolvency Backstop (verified against the paper)

The core numbers in Dayen's article trace to the primary source, [Granato and Drall, "Private Credit's State Backstop"](../bibliography/2026-drall-granato-private-credit-state-backstop.md), verified from the full paper text:

- **49.5%** of new PE-owned insurers' investment was in privately-placed instruments in 2024, versus **13.9%** for independent insurers (Granato & Drall, citing Huber et al. 2026).
- Guaranty funds cover up to **$300,000 of life-insurance benefits** and **$250,000 in present value of annuity benefits** per insured; assessments on surviving insurers are capped at **2% of premium volume per year** and apportioned by premium-volume share.
- The paper estimates that **private-credit default rates above ~15%** could render insolvent insurers with $350–400 billion in general-account assets; the threshold falls as private credit grows as a share of assets.
- **No major life insurer insolvency has ever been mediated through the guaranty-fund system.** The largest liquidation (Executive Life, 1991) produced total assessments of only **$3.7 billion**. AIG, with **over $1 trillion** in assets in 2008, required a federal bailout of **over $100 billion** (TARP plus credit facilities) — a scale that would make simultaneous multi-state guaranty-fund management "uncharted and potentially disastrous."
- The authors' reforms: replace ex-post, premium-volume-based assessments with **ex-ante, risk-calibrated assessments funded like the FDIC's deposit insurance fund**; assess corporate parents (including PE firms) for affiliated-insurer insolvencies; impose procedural penalties on private-credit valuation opacity; scrutinize rating agencies; and give enhanced review to affiliated transactions.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This entry documents the **state-funded downside of the AI credit cycle** — the institutional counterpart to the off-balance-sheet financing documented in the [hidden AI debts evidence entry](2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md). That entry shows hyperscalers pushing ~$1.65tn of data-center obligations into project-finance/SPV structures funded by private-credit funds, insurers, and banks (what the BIS calls "shadow borrowing"); this entry identifies the ultimate loss-absorber if that debt defaults: life insurers backstopped by state guaranty funds and tax credits, i.e., state taxpayers.

It also complements the [Microsoft capex evidence entry](2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md): the capex that nearly doubled is financed partly by instruments whose downside is state-guaranteed. The 2026 AI-market events (Aschenbrenner -67%, Nasdaq correction) are the leading indicators of the stress the mechanism would be called upon to absorb.

## Limits

- **AI-market figures are as-reported, not independently re-verified.** The Aschenbrenner, Nasdaq, SpaceX, and jurisdiction-count figures come from Dayen's article citing CNBC, WSJ, The Motley Fool, and Heatmap; this entry did not re-fetch those underlying reports.
- **The paper is not AI-specific.** Granato and Drall analyze the PE-life-insurer-private-credit model generally; the AI connection is Dayen's application. The mechanism's claims about data-center loans and mass default are Dayen's framing of the paper's general argument, not the paper's own findings.
- **The ~15% insolvency threshold is a stress-test estimate** (from studies the paper cites), not a realized event; the authors explicitly note no large insurer has failed under the guaranty-fund regime, and the paper's empirical magnitudes come from unpublished working papers.
- **State-by-state heterogeneity is large:** assessment caps, coverage limits, crediting timelines, and even coverage of unallocated annuities vary across the 50 states; national figures aggregate divergent rules.
