# China's AI+ Initiative and Global AI Governance Action Plan: State-Directed Open-Source Diffusion as Strategy

## What This Is

On August 26, 2025, China's State Council published the central policy document of Beijing's current AI strategy — the **"AI+ Initiative" (Guofa [2025] No. 11)** — a ten-year, whole-of-nation directive to integrate AI across the economy and society. It sets hard targets: penetration of new-generation smart terminals and intelligent agents above **70% by 2027** across six key sectors, **90% by 2030**, and a fully formed "intelligent economy and smart society" supporting "socialist modernization" by **2035**. Its companion document, the **Global AI Governance Action Plan**, was released at the World AI Conference on July 26, 2025, and frames AI as "an international public good" and "a new frontier in human development."

The strategy's distinctive feature for this repository is that **open source is state policy, not corporate posture**. Section 11 directs the promotion "of a thriving open-source ecosystem" with "internationally influential open-source projects," open-source contributions to count toward university credit and faculty evaluation, and an open-source technology system "open to the world." Premier Li Qiang's mid-2025 public line — "China's innovation is open and open-source" — turned this into diplomatic positioning against the US proprietary-er front.

## The Headline Positions

- **Adoption-over-frontier orientation.** The directive prioritizes deployment ("applied everywhere") over frontier scale: the USCC's [analysis of the strategy](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/Two_Loops--How_Chinas_Open_AI_Strategy_Reinforces_Its_Industrial_Dominance.pdf) reads this as a deliberate bet that deployment generates the proprietary, real-world data that "no amount of web scraping or synthetic generation can replicate" — the physical-economy feedback loop.
- **Copyright and data position (section 8)**: "refine data property rights and copyright systems adapted to AI development," encourage "value contribution–based data cost compensation and revenue sharing," and promote the lawful release of copyrighted content generated by publicly funded projects — a *state-authored* answer to the very corpus-ownership questions the US is litigating ([LibGen/MDL](../evidence/legal/2026-openai-copyright-md-l-chatgpt-logs-discovery.md), [Bartz](../evidence/legal/2026-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement.md)).
- **Global posture.** The Global AI Governance Action Plan (MFA, [July 2025](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/zy/gb/202507/t20250729_11679232.html)) supports "the U.N. as the main channel" for governance, "respecting national sovereignty," and — per [Premier Li Qiang at WAIC](https://www.ussc.edu.au/intelligent-everything-china-s-policy-to-supercharge-ai-adoption) — warns against AI becoming the "exclusive game" of "a few countries or companies," a transparent reference to the US proprietary labs, while proposing a new World AI Cooperation Organisation for the Global South.

## Interpretation: The Third Sovereign, Sailing the Open Source as Standard-Setting

The [territories model](../model/territories-of-the-ai-frontier.md) predicted the actors in this contest would "rotate between pirate and sovereign postures." China's AI+ is that rotation made into national doctrine: **Beijing is the pirate-champion of the open deep-seas** — pushing open weights, open datasets, and open-source ecosystems globally — **while retaining enclosure at the domestic frontier** ("security and controllability," the CAC's [AI technical-standards framework for open-source risk](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/Two_Loops--How_Chinas_Open_AI_Strategy_Reinforces_Its_Industrial_Dominance.pdf)). The pattern matches [Durand and Vergne's](../bibliography/2010-durand-vergne-organisation-pirate.md) description of pirates who "contest those boundaries," but with the state itself as the boundary-tester against the US-led legitimate order — while simultaneously codifying its own (copyright systems "adapted to AI development").

Read against its competitors, the AI+ Initiative is the clearest expression of **_diffusion as dominance_**: where the [US Action Plan](2025-us-ai-action-plan-global-dominance.md) exports an American standardized (including open-weight) stack to allies, and the [EU AI Continent plan](2025-eu-ai-continent-action-plan-sovereignty.md) codifies a governed European space premised on the vulnerability that "the EU currently lags behind the US and China," China's bet is inverse — open the frontier to global diffusion, then harvest the deployment data. Each bloc's document supplies its own [J-curve](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) timing: for the US, the race must be won before the grid becomes the bottleneck; for the EU, the lag is reported as present tense; for China, the 2027/2030/2035 targets are the state committing its own timeline.

## Limits

- **Strategy documents, not measurements.** The AI+ directive and Governance Action Plan state intent; the repo records no verification that the penetration targets are being hit.
- **Rhetoric is tripartisan.** All three bloc documents frame themselves as principled and the others as interest-driven; the entry records each bloc's self-understanding, not an objective ranking.
- **Translation dependence.** The primary text is Chinese-language; quotes here use the CSET and gov.cn English renderings, flagged in access_notes.
- **Single-state view.** The entry captures Beijing's position; it should be read with the [US entry](2025-us-ai-action-plan-global-dominance.md) and [EU entry](2025-eu-ai-continent-action-plan-sovereignty.md) for the three-sided contest the model claims.
