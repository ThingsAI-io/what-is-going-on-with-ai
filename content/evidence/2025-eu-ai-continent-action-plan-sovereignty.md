# EU AI Continent Action Plan: Sovereignty as the European Answer to the US-China AI Race

## What This Is

On April 9, 2025, the European Commission adopted **the "AI Continent Action Plan" (COM(2025)165)** — the EU's strategic answer to the US and China in the contest this repository tracks. Its first line commits the union "to become a global leader in Artificial Intelligence, a leading AI continent," and its framing sentence for urgency is that in this domain, "the race for leadership in AI is far from over."

Where the US strategy ([see the AI Action Plan entry](2025-us-ai-action-plan-global-dominance.md)) is offensive — win the race, export the stack, contain the adversary — the European plan is defensive-codifying: it spends to build European infrastructure, but its distinctive contribution is the governed, rights-based boundary. The AI Act is retained as the trust-and-safety line, while the plan's five strategic domains reconstruct capability underneath it: computing infrastructure, data, skills, algorithms and adoption, and regulatory simplification.

## The Headline Numbers and Positions

- **€200 billion mobilized** through InvestAI (announced at the Paris AI Action Summit, Feb 2025).
- **Infrastructure stack**: at least 13 AI Factories operational by 2026; up to five **AI Gigafactories** (each ~100,000 state-of-the-art AI chips, four times current factories); **€20 billion** of InvestAI for gigafactory investment.
- **Cloud and AI Development Act**: at least triple EU data-center capacity in five-seven years, prioritizing sustainable facilities.
- **Sovereignty claim**: the Communication states bluntly that the EU "currently lags behind the US and China in terms of available data centre capacity, relying heavily on infrastructure installed in and controlled by other regions of the world" whose dominance is "an economic security risk," and that critical use cases require "sovereignty and operational autonomy" on "highly secure EU-based cloud capacity."
- **Adoption gap**: "Only 13.5% of EU companies use AI" — the target the Apply AI Strategy addresses.
- **Semiconductor autonomy**: the Chips Act is pursued for "strategic autonomy in the design and production of AI semiconductors," supported by the DARE initiative (€240 million) toward RISC-V-based sovereign HPC.

## Interpretation: Codifying the Boundaries Before the Pirates Settle Them

Through the [boundary-testing](../frameworks/boundary-testing.md) lens, the AI Continent Action Plan is the **European state-machine drawing the boundary before the pirates arrive** — the mirror image of the US posture. The US tries to win the frontier and then export the standard; the EU tries to codify the frontier first (AI Act risk-tiers, data sovereignty, quality labels) and build the capability underneath. This is the [territories model's](../model/territories-of-the-ai-frontier.md) third move, "new boundary formation," executed declaratively: value is denied to US and Chinese "pirates" not by export controls but by re-territorializing the European data and infrastructure space ([the data-union-as-territory reading of the framework](../frameworks/boundary-testing.md)).

The plan's treatment of data is its sharpest territorial move: a **European Data Union Strategy** reclassifies European data as an internal resource to be pooled and curated in "data labs" inside AI factories — enclosure of the corpus at the level of the continent, in parallel to the [corpus-enclosure litigation](../evidence/legal/2026-openai-copyright-md-l-chatgpt-logs-discovery.md) playing out in US courts. The contrast with the US is exact: the US leaves the corpus open and fights over export controls; the EU gates the corpus and exports standards instead.

The plan also quantifies the [J-curve lag](../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): the whole document is premised on the gap between European adoption (13.5%) and American and Chinese deployment — the state responding in 2025 to a technical curve that began moving years earlier. It pairs with the repo's record of enforcement lag ([the annulled Garante fine](../evidence/legal/2026-rome-tribunal-annuls-garante-openai-fine.md), the [AI Act enforcement letter](../evidence/declarations/2026-eu-ai-act-enforcement-letter.md)) as the two-sided European story: ambition declared, enforcement slow.

## Limits

- **A plan, not a balance sheet.** €200bn "mobilized"/"aimed at" is an ambition; committed versus delivered funding is not measured in the Communication.
- **Same-source blind spots.** The Commission credits the AI Act with boosting trust and competitiveness symmetrically; the entry records the claim, not the verdict.
- **Two-sided evidence needed.** For a complete picture pair this entry with the [US AI Action Plan](2025-us-ai-action-plan-global-dominance.md) and [China's AI+ Initiative](2025-china-ai-plus-action-open-source.md); none of the three documents observes the other two's capabilities disinterestedly.
- **No outcome data.** Whether gigafactories, the Data Union, and the triple-capacity buildout occurred is outside this document's four corners.
