# Encyclopaedia Britannica and Merriam-Webster v. OpenAI: ~100,000 Articles, Collective Registrations, and Hallucination Claims

## What This Is

On March 13, 2026, **Encyclopaedia Britannica, Inc.** and its subsidiary **Merriam-Webster, Inc.** sued nine OpenAI entities in the Southern District of New York (1:26-cv-02097). The complaint, filed by **Susman Godfrey**, alleges that OpenAI copied **nearly 100,000 online articles, encyclopedia entries, and dictionary definitions** — covered by **13 collective-work registrations** and 15 registration certificates — to train its GPT models, reproduced the works near-verbatim in outputs, and committed Lanham Act violations by allowing ChatGPT to **falsely attribute hallucinated answers** to the Britannica and Merriam-Webster marks. The case was associated with the consolidated OpenAI copyright MDL (25-md-3143) and **stayed on April 21, 2026** pending the MDL's summary-judgment motions.

## The Claims

1. **Reproduction for training and RAG:** The complaint alleges the works were copied both to train the models and to be retrieved and used in retrieval-augmented generation and grounding systems.
2. **Derivative/reproduced outputs:** Near-verbatim output examples include Merriam-Webster's dictionary definition of **"plagiarize"**, Britannica's **"Education"** and **"Tourism"** articles, and Britannica's ordered **Hamilton-Burr duel list**.
3. **Lanham Act false attribution:** Hallucinations and undisclosed omissions supposedly attributed to the trusted reference marks.
4. **Harm:** AI-generated summaries "cannibalize" the web traffic and subscription/advertising revenue the reference publishers would otherwise receive.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is the reference-content pillar of the corpus: the plaintiffs are brands whose distinctive value is *trustedness*, not mere text. The case brings the [Liang and Lu](../../bibliography/2026-liang-creative-ownership-ai.md) counterfactual criterion into sharp relief — the "plagiarize"-definition output and the near-verbatim encyclopedia entries are the strongest pleaded evidence that outputs essentially depend on particular training works. It also pairs the copyright theory with **trademark harm** (hallucinated attribution), an angle the repo has not otherwise tracked and that does not depend on proving output similarity — a complement to the CMI/attribution theory in [Doe v. GitHub](2026-doe-v-github-copilot-open-source-cmi.md).

Procedurally, the stay pending MDL summary-judgment decisions makes this case a bellwether sensor: its fate will largely be decided by the [OpenAI copyright MDL](2026-openai-copyright-md-l-chatgpt-logs-discovery.md) dispositions, which the repo already tracks. The case is a textbook instance of the [Durand and Vergne](../../bibliography/2010-durand-vergne-organisation-pirate.md) boundary-testing pattern — a rights-holder with a uniquely valuable corpus invoking the state's machinery to determine whether mass ingestion plus attribution-threatening output crosses the line.

## Limits

- Everything here is **alleged, not adjudicated**; the complaint's near-verbatim examples and corpus counts are pleaded claims.
- The case is **stayed** and may be substantially reshaped by MDL summary judgment rulings.
- The collective-work registrations and the "plagiarize" example were verified from the complaint facsimile and docket; the precise corpus count (~100,000) is the plaintiffs' allegation.
