# Vacker v. ElevenLabs: First Settlement of the AI-Voice Copyright Wave (Confidential)

## What This Is

*Vacker v. ElevenLabs, Inc.* (1:24-cv-00987-RGA, D. Del.), filed **August 29, 2024**, by voice actors **Karissa Vacker** and **Mark Boyett** — later joined by audiobook authors including Jennifer Larson and Iron Tower Press — settled on **confidential terms** after mediation on **August 18, 2025**. The case alleged that ElevenLabs cloned the actors' voices — distributed under the names **"Bella"** and **"Adam"** — consensually for one narrator, then used the voices to generate complete audiobooks, animating the narrators' right-of-publicity/misappropriation claims together with **DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention** and **§1202 CMI-removal** theories. The docket was terminated on **November 6, 2025**.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

The settlement closed the first of the AI-voice cases, and the template matters more than the (secret) number: the claims combined **state right-of-publicity** law with **DMCA §1201/§1202** — the same two statutory highways the repo's scraping cases travel ([Reddit v. SerpApi](2026-reddit-v-serpapi-perplexity-dmca-scraping.md), [Doe v. GitHub](2026-doe-v-github-copilot-open-source-cmi.md)) — applied to a *voice model* rather than a text or code model. It is the ai-economy counterpart to [Bartz v. Anthropic](2026-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement.md): a confidential terms settlement that prices the boundary without adjudicating it, exactly the "settle rather than decide" channel the repo tracks, and a contrast to the litigated paths of [GEMA v. OpenAI](2025-gema-v-openai-lyrics-memorization.md) and [GEMA v. Suno](2026-gema-v-suno-music-verdict.md).

The voice itself is the irreducible creative asset here: the settlement is a market answer, in the [Liang and Lu](../../bibliography/2026-liang-creative-ownership-ai.md) register, to whether a performer's distinct vocal identity retains essential commercial value even when no recorded work as such is verbatim reproduced — a heavy-tailed output domain where the frontier work (a recognizable voice) does not dissipate as training corpora grow.

## Limits

- The settlement terms are **confidential**; no monetary figure is public.
- The case settled without adjudication; no precedent on voice cloning or DMCA §1201/§1202 scope was made.
- Subsequent BIPA-based voice suits (2026) against ElevenLabs and others were filed separately and remain pending; this settlement does not resolve them.
