# In re OpenAI Copylit MDL: News Plaintiffs Move for Sanctions Over Alleged Discovery Obstruction

## What This Is

In the consolidated *In re OpenAI, Inc. Copyright Infringement Litigation* (25-md-3143-SHS-OTW, S.D.N.Y.), on **July 9, 2026**, The New York Times and roughly **17 other news organizations** moved for sanctions against OpenAI, alleging a **"deliberate and systematic effort to obstruct discovery"** spanning about two years. The motion followed the discovery orders already recorded in this repo (the 20-million-log sample, additional 78M/10M log reservoirs, and the Monaco 30(b)(6) deposition).

## The Allegations

- **Concealed search tools:** OpenAI allegedly concealed the tools it uses to search its own training data and ChatGPT output logs.
- **Unusable sample:** The court reportedly deemed OpenAI's **20-million-log sample "unusable"** because of **mass redactions**.
- **Deletions:** Reuters reported that **"billions" of ChatGPT conversations** were deleted in the dispute's window.
- **Evasive deposition:** The deposition of employee **John "Vinnie" Monaco** allegedly contradicted OpenAI's representations about the searchability of its data.
- **Remedies sought:** bar OpenAI from using the logs; a jury instruction of **adverse inference**; restrictions on argument that outputs involve "no substantial copying"; and fees and costs.

The motion had been **briefed but not decided** as of the access date; OpenAI disputes the characterization.

## Why It Matters for This Repository

This is the enforcement tail of the discovery saga recorded in [20 Million ChatGPT Logs](2026-openai-copyright-md-l-chatgpt-logs-discovery.md). The [OpenAI copyright MDL](2026-openai-copyright-md-l-chatgpt-logs-discovery.md) — the evidentiary engine of the copyright frontier — has moved from *what* OpenAI must produce to *whether it produced it and hid tools to avoid doing so*. A sanctions ruling (or settlement) will directly affect every other entry in this subfolder that depends on the logs as the empirical base for whether outputs reproduce training works — the [Liang and Lu](../../bibliography/2026-liang-creative-ownership-ai.md) criterion, the near-verbatim output evidence in [Britannica v. OpenAI](2026-britannica-merriam-webster-v-openai.md), and the discovery framing of the MDL merits.

For the [Durand and Vergne](../../bibliography/2010-durand-vergne-organisation-pirate.md) and [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) threads, the motion is evidence that the state's machinery is not only slow but is now *forcing* the disclosures the frontier disputes, and that non-cooperation itself is becoming the contested act — the boundary-testing shifted from training-data access to what training data the lab will admit to possessing.

## Limits

- This is an **allegation in a pending motion**; nothing is adjudicated, and OpenAI denies wrongdoing.
- Coverage relies on Bloomberg Law/Reuters/Law360 reports of the obtained motion rather than the motion's own text (flagged in access_notes).
- "Billions" of deleted conversations is a reported Reuters figure, not a court finding.
