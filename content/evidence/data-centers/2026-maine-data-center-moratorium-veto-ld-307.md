# Maine Is the First State to Pass a Data Center Moratorium; Governor Mills Vetoes It Over the Town of Jay Exemption (LD 307, Apr 2026)

## What This Is

In the 2026 session, Maine became the first state to produce a statewide-in-scope data center moratorium bill through a legislature. **LD 307**, "An Act to Establish the Maine Data Center Coordination Council and Place a Temporary Limitation on Certain Data Centers," would have:

- imposed a **moratorium on new data center permits for facilities ≥20MW** until **Nov 1, 2027** (with an optional one-year extension);
- created a **13-member Data Center Coordination Council** to study the industry's impacts and report to the Legislature by **Feb 1, 2027**.

The bill passed with bipartisan support. It followed two years of local moratoria (at least four towns in 2024–25) driven by concern about water, grid, and land use across rural Maine communities — the same concern categories that dominate the [Brockovich map numbers](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md).

## The Veto

On **April 24, 2026**, Governor Janet Mills vetoed LD 307. Her stated grounds: the bill contained **no exemption for the Town of Jay project** — a negotiated development next to a paper mill that she says her administration spent roughly two years working on with the developer and town, citing **~450 to ~1,000 jobs** plus "a substantial stream of property taxes" to Jay. She argues the project is in a "large, rare and deed-restricted property" designed to use hydropower in a way that poses "essentially none of the concerns associated with typical data centers," so a flat moratorium would kill it. Mills pledged instead to **form a commission by executive order** to study the state's approach.

On the tax side, the same session produced **LD 713**, which removes data centers from the eligibility for the state's business equipment tax incentive program. Governor Mills **signed** LD 713.

## Interpretation: The Moratorium-Movement Inflection Point

Maine illustrates the two-step pattern now repeating across states: **(1) a legislature passes a pause** — riding the broad, bipartisan anti-data-center sentiment this repo quantifies via [Gallup in the backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md) and the [$64B block/delay tally](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) — and **(2) an executive tries to carve out a specific negotiated project**. The carve-out question is precisely the tension that later turned political in [Box Elder County, Utah](2026-utah-stratos-oleary-data-center-backlash.md), where MIDA's fast-tracked approval (without public transparency) became an election issue.

The veto keeps the episode from becoming the first *enacted* statewide moratorium — a role New York filled on **July 14, 2026**, when Governor Hochul signed an executive order creating the first statewide moratorium on new hyperscale data centers (see the [backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md)). Maine's status, then, is: *first state legislature to pass a statewide-scope moratorium; first governor to replace it with a study commission.*

## Limits

- **Not enacted.** The moratorium never became law; the empirical change is the veto plus the tax-incentive repeal, not a frozen permitting calendar.
- **Job figures are the governor's claim.** The ~450–1,000 job number and the negotiating history come from the veto message itself (partisan source), not an independent audit.
- **Secondary confirmation for LD 713 mechanics.** The bill number and signing were reconstructed from Maine Public/legislative-tracker coverage at access; the veto letter is the firmly primary element.
- **Small-state phenomenon.** Maine's data center industry is nascent; the bill is a signal of political contagion more than a measured industry constraint.
