# France's Data-Center Backlash Spills Into Municipal Elections, Moratoria Calls, and Legal Challenges

## What This Is

In March 2026, Reuters reported that the backlash against data centres had become a live issue in French municipal politics. In Le Bourget, north of Paris, green-ticket candidate Sofiane Milous was campaigning to reverse a planned data centre; Reuters quoted him saying the project would intensify heat islands, worsen noise pollution and create few local jobs. The article also said a Reuters tally found candidates in **at least 10 towns** - including **Marseille** and **Bordeaux** - campaigning against new data centres or calling for moratoria or more transparency.

The French case matters because the opposition is not just protest. It is entering municipal election platforms, administrative conditions, and court filings.

## The Direct Quotes

Reuters' language is blunt:

> "We lost an industry that gave us a livelihood, even if it polluted, and now we face this new 'industry 4.0' that doesn't create jobs for residents," Milous told Reuters.

At the Le Bourget market, a resident explained the local version of the objection:

> "I signed the petition - it's right next to schools. I want a park for my children; we have no green space."

And the article's broader framing is explicit:

> "This mirrors trends across Europe and the United States, where a surge in data centre construction has fuelled a backlash and turned power use and Big Tech's footprint into electoral issues on a local and national level."

## What the Reuters Tally Shows

- Le Bourget is the most developed local example: a candidate running against the project, a petition, and a state approval that still required additional studies and a public meeting.
- Marseille shows the issue is not limited to Paris suburbs: the city is a data-centre hotspot and a left-wing candidate called for a moratorium.
- Wissous shows the legal form of pushback: a municipal candidate was bringing a court challenge against an Amazon data-centre extension.
- The article's tally of at least 10 towns suggests a national political diffusion, not an isolated NIMBY fight.

## Interpretation: The European Version of the Social-License Problem

This is the same structural problem documented in the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): the technical project exists, but the social and regulatory complements needed to make it go live are missing. In the US this shows up as county commissions, referendum drives, and ballot bans; in France it shows up as municipal campaigns, prefectural conditions, and administrative appeals.

The French state is not anti-data-centre. It is trying to attract investment while demanding process and conditions. Reuters notes that France is promoting nuclear power as a data-centre draw and that a draft law would classify data centres as projects of national interest, reducing local veto points. That is the same growth-vs-backlash tension seen in the US, but mediated through French administrative law.

## Limits

- **This is mostly municipal politics, not a national opinion poll.** Reuters' tally captures campaign activity, not a countrywide survey.
- **The project landscape is uneven.** Some sites are planned, some approved, some challenged; the local institutional stage differs town by town.
- **Reuters direct access was blocked.** The text here relies on Reuters search snippets and syndicated republishing, but the quotes above are verbatim from the Reuters copy.
