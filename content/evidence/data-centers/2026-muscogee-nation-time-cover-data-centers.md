# Two Muscogee Women Land on the Cover of TIME ("The People vs. AI") After Stopping a Hyperscale Data Center on Tribal Land

## What This Is

Two citizens of the Muscogee (Creek) Nation — **Jordan Harmon** (33, policy specialist at the Indigenous Environmental Network) and **Mackenzie Roberts** (27, intake specialist at the Muscogee Nation Center for Victim Services) — are the face of TIME's March 9, 2026 cover story, **"The People vs. AI"**, about Americans pushing back against the AI infrastructure boom.

The contested project: a 2025 bill by the tribal legislature to rezone **"Looped Square Ranch"** — **5,500 acres in Okmulgee County, Oklahoma** the Nation bought in 2021 for food security, hunting, fishing, and cultural preservation (a working cattle operation) — into a technology park (the **"Mvskoke Tech Park"**) hosting a hyperscale AI data center. Harmon and Roberts responded by staging town halls across the county, arguing the rezoning would put water wells at risk in an area already plagued by boil orders, trade healthy land and food sovereignty for an extractive use, and fail to explain who pays for upgraded water infrastructure.

## The Result and Its Framing

The **Muscogee National Council rejected the proposal in November 2025**. Representative Dode Barnett, who voted no after watching the women's town halls livestreamed, put it plainly: "The amount of money that they're saying we could make is a large amount of money. But it's also going to leave our land scarred, and we can't ever get that back. This feels like a modern-day land run."

The two women's framing connects the data center fight to longer histories:

- **Harmon:** "They need these data centers for their programs like AI facial recognition to surveil and police people, including Indigenous people... This is all a part of the legacy of colonialism and imperialism."
- **Roberts:** "Data center development is like another iteration of American imperialism and colonization. We have already been historically deemed as sacrifice zones."

TIME's story carries the same arc the [Brockovich map](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md) quantifies — water, land, self-determination — while grounding it in tribal sovereignty and food security. Roberts has since advised other tribes, helped lead a Seminole Nation town hall, and joined the "Stop Data Colonialism" coalition; the **Seminole Nation passed a moratorium on its own land (March 2026)**, and the **Cherokee Nation created a data-center impact task force (February 2026)**.

## Where the Muscogee Fight Sits in the National Pattern

This entry is the indigenous-sovereignty node of the broader backlash the repository tracks:

- The [bipartisan backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md) shows 70% of Americans opposed to local construction; for tribes, that objection accrues additional weight — the land is treaty-protected, the water is already stressed, and "food deserts everywhere" (Roberts) make agriculture replacement unacceptable.
- The [Data Center Watch $64B tally](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) counts project-years lost to local opposition; the Muscogee rejection is one such loss, secured not by a ban but by tribal governance.
- The surrounding Oklahoma picture — Google's 827-acre Project Spring in Sand Springs adjacent to Osage land, Meta's Project Anthem within Muscogee reservation boundaries, and tribes weighing revenue against sovereignty — is the current frontier the KOSU reporting (Jun 2026) documents "as data centers eye Oklahoma."
- The momentum reverses the [US state-driven buildout framing](../2025-us-ai-action-plan-global-dominance.md) noted elsewhere in the repo: the federal government has been actively encouraging tribes to partner with data center developers.

## Limits

- **A single, localized win.** The National Council rejection covers one 5,500-acre parcel; new projects (Google's, Meta's) continue within or adjacent to tribal territory.
- **Coverage quality varies.** TIME's feature is the canonical source but paywalled; the verified record here rests on Native News Online, Crosswinds News, KOSU, and TIME excerpts.
- **Framing is the subjects' own.** Colonialism/imperialism characterizations are the activists' framing (correctly attributed), not findings of fact.
