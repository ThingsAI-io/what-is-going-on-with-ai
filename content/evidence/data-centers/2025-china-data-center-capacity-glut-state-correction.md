# China's Data-Center Boom Turns Into a State-Corrected Capacity Glut, Not an Open Public Backlash

## What This Is

Reuters reported in July 2025 that China was moving to build a network to sell surplus computing power after a three-year data-center boom created a **capacity glut**. The core facts in the Reuters report are not about street protests or municipal rebellion. They are about **state correction**: a nationwide review by the state planner, a state-run cloud proposal, tighter rules from the NDRC, and state telecoms being pulled into the cleanup.

The strongest quoted voice in the Reuters package is Chen Yili, deputy chief engineer at the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, who said the government wanted users to be able to hand workloads to a central cloud service. As Reuters relayed it:

> "Everything will be handed over to our cloud for unified organization, orchestration, and scheduling capabilities."

## The Overbuild Problem

Reuters said the building boom had produced a system with:

- **at least 7,000 licensed computing centers** since 2022;
- **24.7 billion yuan** in state investment in 2024, and **12.4 billion yuan** in 2025 to date;
- utilization rates of only **20%-30%**;
- **over 100 cancellations** over the previous 18 months.

That is not a backlash in the US sense. It is a policy failure that Beijing is trying to centralize away.

The Reuters text also says the NDRC has already tightened scrutiny on new projects planned after March 20 and banned local governments from participating in small computing infrastructure projects.

## Interpretation: China Has a Different Constraint

China's visible constraint is not open local opposition. It is **overbuild, low utilization, and administrative consolidation**. The state is not being forced to back down by voters; it is reclassifying the problem as a coordination failure.

That is why this belongs alongside the [France backlash entry](2026-france-data-center-backlash-municipal-elections.md) and the US cases in this folder, but as a different mechanism. France shows the democratic version of siting resistance. China shows the planned-economy version of the same structural problem: if the capacity is built too fast, the state has to step in and manage the glut.

This also fits the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): the infrastructure is there before the complementary demand, distribution, and scheduling systems are ready. In China, the corrective is top-down consolidation rather than bottom-up protest.

## Limits

- **No strong evidence of open public backlash found here.** The most visible Chinese signal is overcapacity and state correction, not protests.
- **The Reuters article is a report, not a government release.** The figures come from Reuters' reconstruction using sources familiar with the matter and a document seen by Reuters.
- **The state may still choose scale over efficiency.** Later Chinese policy can still favor buildout; this entry only pins the 2025 correction moment.
