# Quantica's 5,000-Acre "Big Sky Campus" Near Broadview, Montana: A Private-Equity-Backed 1GW AI Data Center Meets Rancher Resistance

## What This Is

Just south of Broadview, Montana (population ~130, ~31 miles north of Billings), oil-and-gas private equity firm **EnCap Investments** built a land position of **~5,000 acres (~8 square miles)** starting in 2024 via shell entity Montana Property LLC, then announced in **July 2025** the creation of **Quantica Infrastructure** to develop it into the **"Big Sky Campus"** — an AI-ready data center and renewable-energy park with **500MW in phase 1, scaling to 1GW** (more power than NorthWestern Energy supplies to all of Montana on an average day). The NYT (May 26, 2026) built its feature around **Kassi Solberg**, a rancher who kept showing up to Broadview's tiny town council meetings demanding information about "a behemoth 5,000-acre A.I. data center... the size of about 3,800 football fields."

When Broadview's mayor declined to host a public forum — on the grounds that the site lies outside town limits, "so it doesn't affect us" — Solberg's reply became the article's emotional core: "It affects your residents!"

## The Developer's Model

Quantica's pitch is different from the [Utah Stratos case](2026-utah-stratos-oleary-data-center-backlash.md) in one important respect: it argues it *pays its own way*. The company says it will power the campus with **on-site wind, solar, and battery storage**, use **closed-loop cooling from deep aquifers**, and connect to the grid via a new ~$25–30 million transmission line (21 miles) to NorthWestern Energy, which has signed a letter of agreement to supply up to **1,000 megawatts**. The carbon footprint argument — "bring your own power" allowing AI infrastructure to bypass grid interconnection queues — is the developer's answer to the [grid-capacity concerns that top the Brockovich map](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md).

## Facts and Claims in Dispute

- **The land-and-power lawsuit.** Well-known Montana businessman Rick Tabish (FX Solutions) is suing EnCap/Broad Reach Power/Quantica in state court, alleging he was tricked — promised power in exchange for selling ~350 acres around the strategically vital Broadview substation, then cut out and competed against. His complaint: "This case is about how a Texas private equity fund duped a loyal Montana businessman... and is now building its own data center." The parties dispute the figures are "worth billions — plural."
- **The substation politics.** FX Solutions/NorthWestern litigated the same substation-area power arrangements in federal court (2022–2025); NorthWestern then pivoted to *contracting* with Quantica's affiliate Broadview Solar — the very utility whose predecessor it fought.
- **Tax posture.** Montana's property tax rate below 1% is a stated reason data centers are coming; residents like Shawn Olson question "whether a billion-dollar industry needs less than one percent property tax."

## Interpretation: Land Economics vs. Community Consent in the Great Plains

Quantica/Big Sky shows the second distinct pattern in the national backlash: **the developer's supply-side answer ("on-site renewables, closed-loop cooling") does not neutralize the demand-side grievance ('my home, my water, my grid, my town')**. The tension between the [Brockovich map's water/grid categories](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md) and Quantica's self-description ("hydropower-alternative, aquifer-based closed loop") is exactly where local suspicion lives — and the FX/Quantica lawsuit puts a private-equity land-grab charge at the center that no technical brochure answers.

The mixed institutional response — a state energy-siting bill (HB 799) whose wind-farm spacing rules were co-written with the wind industry, a 2026 lawsuit, a federal court fight over the transmission line — parallels the institutional-lag pattern of the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md): the physical buildout is funded, but community assent is not pre-purchasable with infrastructure design.

## Limits

- **Project unbuilt.** Big Sky is in early construction (leveling/roads began 2026); all "impact" statements are estimates/claims.
- **One-sided Tabs.** The FX/Quantica litigation allegations are the plaintiff's complaint, unadjudicated.
- **Renewable-power claim unverified.** On-site renewables sufficient for 500MW-1GW load, plus deep-aquifer closed-loop cooling, are developer assertions pending environmental review.
- **NYT piece as human-scale window.** The quantitative record is the developer/state filings, not the feature.
