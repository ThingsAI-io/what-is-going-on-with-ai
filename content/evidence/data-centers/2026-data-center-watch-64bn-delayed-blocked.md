# Data Center Watch: $64 Billion in US Data Center Capacity Blocked or Delayed by Organized Local Opposition

## What This Is

Data Center Watch — a project of the AI firm 10a Labs that tracks local data center activity — published its first report (August 2026) quantifying the local opposition to the AI buildout. The headline figure: **$64 billion in US data center projects blocked or delayed in the past two years**, split between **$18B of projects fully stopped** and **$46B materially delayed**. The report attributes this not to market forces but to organized pressure: **at least 142 local activist groups across 24 states**, with Virginia as the focal point (42 groups in the country's largest data center market). The report counts **46 moratoria or ordinances in at least 13 states and 23 cities/areas**.

## The Headline Numbers

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Projects blocked | $18 billion |
| Projects delayed | $46 billion |
| **Total blocked/delayed** | **$64 billion** |
| Activist groups | ≥142, across 24 states |
| States examined | 28 (those with hyperscaler 50MW+ projects) |
| Focal state (groups) | Virginia (42) |
| Local restrictions | 46 moratoria/ordinances in ≥13 states, 23 cities/areas |

The report's chronology begins with the founding of the **Data Center Reform Coalition in 2023** and traces the movement's escalation through 2024, when the first bans landed (Northern Virginia's moratoriums) and local action spread from state to state.

## Interpretation: The Local-Legitimacy Check on the Capital Buildout

Data Center Watch's numbers measure, at the *project* scale, the grievance base first documented as reported *concerns* in the [Brockovich community map](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md). Where that entry tracks resident-reported water, grid, health, and noise complaints, this entry measures the institutional outcome: money that hyperscalers committed (see the [Microsoft capex evidence](../finance-of-ai/2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md) and [Nikkei financing evidence](../finance-of-ai/2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md)) but cannot convert into capacity because local politics will not permit it.

The $64B figure sits inside the same reality the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) predicts: the intangible complements the buildout needs include *social license*, and that license is now being priced — in Virginia's rural-urban divide and in the specifically local cases this repo tracks: the [Stratos/Box Elder County project](2026-utah-stratos-oleary-data-center-backlash.md) (approved over hundreds of protesters and now subject to a referendum drive), the [Quantica/Broadview campus](2026-montana-rancher-big-sky-campus.md), and the [Muskogee Nation's rejection](2026-muscogee-nation-time-cover-data-centers.md). The [bipartisan backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md) explains why this opposition spans both parties.

## Limits

- **Definitional call on "blocked vs. delayed."** The split is the report's judgment; a project that later proceeds would be reclassified, and "delayed" projects are not necessarily cancelled.
- **Counting method not fully public in the fetched text.** The report's basis for grouping organizations (websites, involvement in local fights) and for attributing project delays *to* opposition (vs. financing, permitting, or demand changes) is not itemized in the publicly visible summary.
- **First edition, uncompared.** As a first report, it has no published methodology change history; its **~$98B blocked/stalled over 3 months of one prior period** (as cited by NBC News, Mar 2026) suggests the series' window definitions have shifted — treat cross-period sums as indicative.
- **Advocate-run and publish-free.** 10a Labs is an AI firm with an anti-data-center stance through this project; the figures are not credentialed like a government registry.
