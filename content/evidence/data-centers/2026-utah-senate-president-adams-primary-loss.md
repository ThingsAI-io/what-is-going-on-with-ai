# Data Center Backlash Topples Utah Senate President Stuart Adams in the 2026 GOP Primary — a US Political First

## What This Is

On **June 23, 2026**, Utah's longest-serving recent Senate President, **Stuart Adams** (R-District 7, Layton), lost his Republican primary to attorney **Stephanie Hollist** (43% vs. 34%, with Braden Hess at 22%). Adams conceded less than two hours after polls closed. Per KSL, it is **the only time in modern Utah history a sitting Senate president or House speaker has lost a primary**.

Adams was the public face of the project this repository documents in the [Stratos/Box Elder data center entry](2026-utah-stratos-oleary-data-center-backlash.md): as **chair of Utah's Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA)** — the fast-track vehicle created to support military installations — he shepherded Kevin O'Leary's **40,000-acre Stratos Project** through approval, complete with 100% property tax relief, before the public learned the details.

## The Numbers Behind the Loss

The immediate after-action data shows the data center was decisive:

- A **Deseret News/Hinckley Institute of Politics poll** (Morning Consult, 850 Utah voters, June 16–22, 2026) found data center opposition at **60%, up from 53% in May**, with the swing driven by Republicans: GOP opposition rose from 36% to 49%, flipping the project's favorability from net +9 points in May to **net −10 points** by the primary.
- **O'Leary himself polled underwater**: 32% unfavorable vs. 26% favorable.
- Adams' late-stage concession — a letter to O'Leary shrinking the project area — was too little: only 24% of voters said it made them more likely to support the project; a majority (53%) said it made no difference.
- Analyst attribution: environmental policy expert **Brigham Daniels**: "what has happened is people in Utah have had a surprisingly strong reaction against the Stratos Project." Consultant **Taylor Morgan**: "I think it really is all about the timing with the data center and Stuart being the target as chair of MIDA."
- **County-level doubling-down**: both Box Elder County commissioners who ratified the project (Boyd Bingham, Lee Perry) lost their GOP primaries days later (~52% and ~54% for their challengers); two other prominent incumbents (Sen. Dan McCay, Rep. Trevor Lee) also lost, though for reasons those same analysts say are less data-center-specific.

## Interpretation: The First Electoral Casualty of the AI Buildout

The Adams primary is the cleanest demonstration yet that the [grievance base mapped nationally](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md) can convert into **jobs held accountable at the ballot box**. Republicans — who control the siting machinery (local commissions and fast-track bodies like MIDA) through which data centers actually get approved — are the political layer now exposed. Utah House Speaker Mike Schultz's verdict is the frankest: "I do blame Kevin O'Leary for this mess... he spent his time lecturing Utahns instead of answering their questions."

This is the electoral component of the institutional-lag strand of the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md), and it is bipartisan: the [bipartisan backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md) shows the same phenomenon measured nationally (Gallup: 70% oppose local construction; 75% Democrats, 63% Republicans). In deep-red Utah, a project championed by the state's most powerful Republican and a television celebrity brought down a 16-year incumbent — an outcome the industry could not buy off. The [Data Center Watch tally](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) of $64B blocked/delayed explains why developers should now treat siting risk as a financing risk: [Microsoft's capex evidence](../finance-of-ai/2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md) assumes the capacity can be built.

## Limits

- **Correlation, not clean experiment.** Adams' loss was multi-causal (he also carried a granddaughter scandal and first-ever primary). The data center was "likely a major factor," not a proven sole cause.
- **Analyst attribution.** "Decisive" is a post-hoc judgment by commentators, consistent with (but not proven by) the polling swing.
- **Single-district N.** One primary is not a political law; the November referendum and 2026 legislative session are the real tests of whether the effect persists.
