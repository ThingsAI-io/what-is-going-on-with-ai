# The Anti-Data-Center Backlash Is Bipartisan: 70% of Americans Oppose Local Construction, and Statehouses of Both Parties Are Responding

## What This Is

In an era of record polarization, opposition to data centers is now a cross-party consensus. **Gallup's May 2026** polling put **~70–71% of Americans against a data center in their area** — with **75% of Democrats and 63% of Republicans** opposed. The counterintuitive detail, reported by Grist: **conservative Republicans (53%) oppose local construction more than moderate Republicans (44%)**, meaning the most tribalist GOP voters sit closest to Democrats on this issue.

This is the polling substrate beneath the events in the rest of the data-centers folder. It explains why the backlash is winning institutional concessions at every level simultaneously.

The breadth is coalitional, not cameo: bans passed in Holyoke (MA), Monterey Park (CA) — by ballot measure — and Seattle (WA) in June 2026 alone; residents of Festus, MO ousted **half their city council** after it approved a $6 billion data center; El Paso held an 8-hour public-comment marathon (~180 speakers) against Meta's 2023 tax abatement; the [Muskogee Nation's National Council](2026-muscogee-nation-time-cover-data-centers.md) rejected a hyperscale project on tribal land in November 2025.

## What's Driving It (and Whether It Lasts)

Grist's analysis, drawing on [Data Center Watch research](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md), says the opposition is not one ideology but a recurring set of local grievances: **electricity bills, water scarcity, noise, land use, tax breaks, distrust of tech billionaires, and the sense that communities are being asked to share resources with an industry that will give little back**. Party-split the concerns tilt differently — Republican officials emphasize **tax incentives and grid strain**; Democrats emphasize **environmental impact and resource consumption** — but both ends land on "slow it down."

The durability question is real. Grist and the NYT both note that data-center backlash is new and unburdened by the political baggage of, say, fracking — which was bipartisan in 2014 and partisan by 2016. Whether 2026's consensus survives the 2026 midterms — or turns into [Utah's primary ouster of a Senate president](2026-utah-senate-president-adams-primary-loss.md) event-driven campaigns — is the open variable.

## Interpretation: The Buildout's Binding Constraint Is Now Social License

The [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) predicts an institutional lag behind technical adoption; this entry measures that lag in polling. But it also shows the lag is *not* passive: voters are converting grievance into law faster than any previous general-purpose-technology wave. The [Brockovich community map](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md) is the reported-concern raw layer; the [Data Center Watch $64B tally](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) is the financial outcome; this entry is the national political frame that makes "64B" and "9,030 reports" legible as policy.

For the finance-of-ai layer, this is a risk input: hyperscalers' [capex commitments (Microsoft, $115.9B FY26)](../finance-of-ai/2026-microsoft-fy26-q4-earnings.md) and [off-balance-sheet project financing (Nikkei, ~$1.65tn)](../finance-of-ai/2026-nikkei-hidden-ai-debt-five-tech-giants.md) presume the capacity can be sited. At 70% public opposition, they are betting against the median American. New York's executive order, Texas' turn, and the $130B of Q1 project losses are the market's leading indicators that the bet is underpriced.

## Limits

- **One poll, one quarter.** Gallup's 70% is a May 2026 snapshot; the "majority of Americans support a national ban" figure (Heatmap) is a different question and method.
- **"Oppose in my area" ≠ "oppose AI."** The majority rejecting local siting is not uniformly anti-AI; some opposition is specifically NIMBY.
- **Local counts are aggregations.** "300+ bans," "142 protests," and "800 groups" come from advocacy-aligned tabulations (The Information, protest organizers, Data Center Watch) with varied rigor.
- **Framing honesty.** "Bipartisan" describes the polling and the local coalitions; state-level politics (esp. party elites' growth coalitions) show real friction, and Texas/Abbott's posture may change after 2026 elections.
