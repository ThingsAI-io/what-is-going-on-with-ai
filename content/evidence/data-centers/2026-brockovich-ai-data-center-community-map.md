# Erin Brockovich's AI Data Center Map: 9,030 Community Reports of Harm Across All 50 States

## What This Is

On April 27, 2026, environmental advocate Erin Brockovich launched a public map — [brockovichdatacenter.com](https://www.brockovichdatacenter.com/) — collecting community reports about AI data centers ("The New Pollution Is Data, and It's Coming to a Town Near You," announced on *The Brockovich Report* Substack the next day, Apr 28, 2026, with reporter Suzanne Boothby). The map is a participatory tool: residents report concerns linked to nearby or planned data centers, and the site publishes running statistics. As of August 16, 2026, it had logged **9,030 reports across all 50 states plus DC and 2,378 zip codes**, including **696 submissions in the last 30 days**.

The site is explicit about its evidentiary status: it is **not** "a registry of every AI data center" and the reports are community concerns, not documented harms. Brockovich's framing in the launch essay is that the map documents "patterns" of growth, conflict, and uncertainty "unfolding town by town" — and that "this is not about attacking AI." The entry's role in this repository is to pin that scale of *reported* local grievance, which downstream shows up as election defeats, moratoria, and blocked projects.

## Interpretation: The Reported-Concern Base Behind the Political Backlash

The map's significance for this repository is not the numbers in isolation but what they sit beneath: the [productivity J-curve](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) predicts institutional lags after adoption, and this entry documents the ground-level grievance base that is now driving the political and regulatory counterweights. The specific localities in the map top-10 are the same places covered by this repo's other data-center evidence entries — the [Stratos Project in Box Elder County, UT](2026-utah-stratos-oleary-data-center-backlash.md) (83 reports; the project that toppled [Utah Senate President Stuart Adams](2026-utah-senate-president-adams-primary-loss.md)), the [Quantica 5,000-acre campus near Broadview, MT](2026-montana-rancher-big-sky-campus.md) opposition (Marshall and Saline, MI and Effingham County, GA recur as locally-reported hotspots), and the [Okmulgee County, OK resistance](2026-muscogee-nation-time-cover-data-centers.md) behind the TIME cover story.

The map's three most common categories — water (41.2%), grid (22.2%), health (18.1%) — match the concern mix that statehouses are now legislating on, and that national polling (Gallup's **70-71% opposition** to local data center construction) is measuring; see the [bipartisan backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md) for those results. As grievance reports became political momentum, [Data Center Watch counted $64B of blocked or delayed projects](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) in the two years to mid-2026 — see that entry for the magnitude the map's reports feed into.

## Limits

- **Unmoderated self-reports, not verified harms.** A "water" complaint is a resident's claim, not a measurement. The map's own disclaimer is the correct evidentiary standard.
- **No denominator.** The site does not normalize reports against local population or number of planned facilities; "Sulphur Springs, TX — 399" could reflect an organizing campaign rather than relative harm.
- **Category overlap.** Percentages are of submissions (multiple categories per submission), not mutually exclusive shares.
- **Short-window volatility.** The last-30-days count (696, or ~7.7% of the total) shows the series is still building, so cross-time comparisons are unstable.
