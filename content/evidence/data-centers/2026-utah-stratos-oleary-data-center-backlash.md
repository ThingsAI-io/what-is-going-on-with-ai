# Kevin O'Leary's $100B "Stratos" Data Center in Box Elder County, Utah: County Approval, Statewide Backlash, and a Referendum Challenge

## What This Is

In 2026, O'Leary Digital — the venture chaired by *Shark Tank* celebrity investor Kevin O'Leary — advanced what it bills as the largest data center project ever proposed in the United States. The **Stratos Project** (also called Wonder Valley) would cover **up to 40,000 acres** in Box Elder County's Hansel Valley, with **7.5–9GW** of on-site power-generating capacity (mostly natural gas), roughly **double Utah's peak electric demand** and more than twice the area of Manhattan. Backers put the price at **$100 billion**; the first phase alone is projected at **more than $4 billion**.

It moved quickly through Utah's unusual **Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA)** — a fast-track economic-development vehicle chaired by Senate President [Stuart Adams](2026-utah-senate-president-adams-primary-loss.md). MIDA approved the project's development zone (including **100% property tax relief**) on **April 24, 2026**; the Box Elder County Commission ratified the final resolutions on **May 4, 2026**, by a 2–1 vote, after hundreds of protesters packed the meeting (Commissioner Boyd Bingham: "For hell's sake, grow up").

## The Backlash

- **Water.** The project's original plan drew from Salt Wells Spring, historically used by Bar H Ranch; after roughly **3,800–4,000 formal objections** public and developers withdrew that application and filed a new one for an unnamed valley spring. A 2026 Utah water-rights change makes it harder for the state engineer to reject applications on public-welfare grounds.
- **Climate/heat.** USU physicist Robert Davies' preliminary analysis estimates a **16GW thermal load** (~23 "atom bombs" equivalent per day), with daytime warming of 2–5°F and nighttime warming of **8–12°F** across the desert valley — a potential dew-point collapse that would further dry the environment. Utah Clean Energy estimated **~448 billion cubic feet of natural gas per year** (1.5x all in-state consumption) and **~30.2 million tons of CO2 per year** — a **55% increase** in Utah's carbon footprint.
- **The Great Salt Lake.** The lake is the emotional and physical epicenter of the fight; opponents fear the project's draws compound the lake's collapse.
- **Democracy/process.** A citizen referendum application (initiated by a group of residents) seeks a November vote to reverse the county approval — an unprecedented test of whether a multibillion-dollar project can be aborted by petition.
- **The developer's response.** O'Leary called opponents "professional protesters that are paid by somebody" and, on Fox News, implied foreign (Chinese) funding of opposition. Under pressure, O'Leary agreed in June 2026 to **reduce the project area by 50%**.

## Post-Approval Government Shifts

The backlash produced real political consequences. By mid-June, a Deseret News/Hinckley Institute of Politics poll (Morning Consult, 850 Utah voters, Jun 16–22, 2026) found **60% of Utah voters opposed** the Stratos Project (up from 53% in May), with opposition among Republicans jumping from 36% to 49%. Gov. Spencer Cox — who had said "I'm so tired of our country taking years to get stuff done" — announced in May he would require **new-phase approvals** (first phase capped at 1.5GW) and, in June, sent O'Leary a letter of conditional support. The electoral consequence followed days later: [Senate President Stuart Adams lost his primary](2026-utah-senate-president-adams-primary-loss.md) on June 23, 2026 — the first sitting Utah Senate president or House speaker to lose a primary in modern history — and both Box Elder commissioners who approved the project lost theirs.

## Interpretation: When Fast-Track Politics Meet the Grievance Base

The Stratos episode is the repository's clearest case of the [J-curve's institutional lag](../../frameworks/productivity-j-curve.md) hitting the *siting* layer of the AI buildout rather than the productivity layer: the capital is real, the permits are moving, but the local political system now behaves as a veto point. The fact that the fight unfolded in a **deep-red county** (Trump ~80% in 2024) — against tax breaks and grid/water externalities — is exactly the cross-partisan configuration measured in the [bipartisan backlash entry](2026-data-center-bipartisan-backlash-gallup.md), and it landed in the same grievance taxonomy as the [Brockovich map](2026-brockovich-ai-data-center-community-map.md), where Box Elder County registers 83 reports dominated by water, grid, health, and "zoning/process" complaints.

The **100% property tax relief** is the specific policy lever that turns siting fights partisan: [Data Center Watch counts](2026-data-center-watch-64bn-delayed-blocked.md) $64B of blocked/delayed projects, and the tax-break framing is central to nearly every case. O'Leary's "foreign agents" framing — echoing claims from the industry's pro-buildout lobby — is the counter-narrative; Fox News' amplification of it is being litigated (Utahns have sued O'Leary and Fox over being called Chinese agents, per The Salt Lake Tribune).

## Limits

- **Approved, not built.** The project remains in permitting; environmental review (Division of Water Rights, Division of Air Quality) is outstanding, so all impact numbers are estimates by critics, not measurements.
- **Scale figures are contested.** The 40,000 acres / 9GW descriptors come from project backers and reporting; the actual footprint depends on tenants and phasing (each phase needs separate approval).
- **Referendum status.** The petition was filed but its certification and the feasibility of reversing an approved special-district resolution were unresolved at access.
- **Partisan polarization risk.** The case shows a single project can be used to demonstrate anti-data-center credibility; whether it *stays* bipartisan (per Grist's analysis) is an open question.
