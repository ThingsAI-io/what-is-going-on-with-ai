# Productivity J-Curve

## In a Nutshell

The productivity J-curve says that when a new general purpose technology arrives, measured productivity often falls before it rises.

The reason is not necessarily that the technology is useless. It is that firms have to build complementary intangible assets before the gains can be realized. Those investments take time, cost money, and often do not show up cleanly in standard measurement.

So the curve bends down first and up later.

## The Core Mechanism

The model has three parts:

1. **Early investment**: firms divert resources into redesign, training, software, and business-process change.
2. **Measurement lag**: the output value of those investments is partly hidden while the input cost is visible.
3. **Later payback**: once the intangible stock matures, the gains show up and measured productivity rises.

This is why the curve is shaped like a J rather than a straight line. The early period is not the opposite of success; it is often the cost of building the conditions for success.

## Why It Matters for AI

AI is a strong candidate for a J-curve because it is not just a tool that can be dropped into existing workflows. It often requires:

- process redesign;
- training and supervision;
- changes to data infrastructure;
- new evaluation routines;
- and revised business models.

Those are exactly the kinds of complementary investments that can suppress measured productivity in the short run while raising it later.

## What the Framework Explains

The J-curve helps explain several recurring AI patterns:

- why adoption can look expensive before it looks profitable;
- why productivity statistics may lag behind visible usage;
- why intangible complements matter as much as the model itself;
- and why a temporary dip should not be mistaken for proof that the technology has no value.

It also helps separate two different questions:

- Is the technology creating future value?
- Are current measurements able to see that value yet?

## Limits

The J-curve is not a guarantee of eventual success.

Some technologies never climb out of the dip. Some organizations fail to build the complements needed for payback. Some gains are captured unevenly, so productivity improves in aggregate while workers or regions lose out.

The framework therefore explains timing and measurement, not destiny.

This repository needs a way to talk about AI productivity that does not confuse temporary adjustment costs with the final state of the system.

The J-curve gives that language.
