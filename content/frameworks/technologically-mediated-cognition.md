# Technologically Mediated Cognition

## In a Nutshell

Some technologies do more than help us express preformed thoughts.

They can become part of the conditions under which thoughts are formed, words are selected, attention is directed, and social relations are enacted.

This framework names that layered phenomenon without pretending that all the underlying theories say the same thing.

## The Shared Insight

The shared insight is simple: AI is not just content.

If it is used repeatedly, routinely, and intimately, it can become a medium that shapes what people notice, how they phrase things, which distinctions feel natural, and how they relate to others through language and action.

That means the technology may not merely assist expression. It may begin to structure expression.

## Four Contributions

1. **McLuhan**: media are extensions of human faculties, and the medium reshapes scale, pace, pattern, and attention.
2. **Clark and Chalmers**: when external resources are reliably coupled to a person and do the relevant cognitive work, cognition can extend into the world.
3. **Vygotsky**: speech originates socially and becomes internalized into verbal thought, so words help form higher mental functions.
4. **Verbeek**: technologies mediate human-world relations and shape practices, perceptions, and decisions.

The framework is the overlap of these claims, not a reduction of them to one sentence.

## Why It Matters for AI

AI systems are especially important because they sit at the junction of cognition, language, and interface.

They can become:

- drafting environments that shape style and tone;
- conversational partners that alter internal speech;
- memory and retrieval aids that change what is salient;
- and media environments that reorganize attention and social interaction.

If those systems are owned and designed by a small number of firms, the cognitive and expressive effects can also become a source of power and rent.

## What the Framework Explains

It helps explain:

- why AI can feel like a personal extension while still being socially and economically structured;
- why style, tone, and phrasing may shift with repeated use;
- why external tools can become habitual parts of thought and work;
- why interface design matters as much as model output;
- and why attention capture is inseparable from expressive capture.

## Limits

The framework should not be read as a claim that AI literally becomes the self.

McLuhan, Clark and Chalmers, Vygotsky, and Verbeek all differ in important ways. Some stress extension, some functional coupling, some internalization, and some mediation. The value of the framework is that it keeps those differences visible while still giving the repository a useful synthesis.

## Relevance to This Repository

This repository is trying to understand how AI changes economy, society, and work.

Technologically mediated cognition is relevant because it gives a careful way to talk about AI as an invasive extension without overclaiming. It connects attention, expression, and world-relation to the economic questions of enclosure, platform power, and rent extraction.
